# Usual Specialists Under Construction — Patch Lockup Image Brief

Status: `completed-awaiting-retirement` — the commissioned image was generated and explicitly accepted on 2026-09-20. The accepted master now lives in repository custody; retire this brief with the next substantive slice under the planning-artifact lifecycle.

## Story job

Create one compact, deliberately charming **under-construction lockup** for *The Usual Specialists* page.

The lockup sits below the latest launched Specialists chapter and holds the line for the unfinished remainder of the story. As later chapters launch, this same section moves down so it always follows the newest completed chapter. It eventually disappears when all six Specialists chapters are live.

The audience should understand immediately:

1. the page is intentionally live in stages rather than accidentally incomplete;
2. more of the Specialists story is coming;
3. Patch is actively working on it;
4. the temporary section still belongs to the authored Adventures of Patch / Usual Specialists world.

This is a light epilogue / holding beat, not a seventh chapter and not a generic website placeholder.

## Commission

One image commission producing **one composed lockup asset**.

The lockup contains:

- canonical Patch;
- a yellow construction hard hat worn over Patch's canonical hood;
- one freestanding construction sign with a large clean sign face;
- one orange traffic cone;
- a short length of striped hazard / barrier tape;
- at most one small secondary work prop such as a rolled plan, clipboard, or compact toolbox if composition needs it.

Do not split this into separately commissioned Patch, sign, cone, or tape assets. The commission is for the assembled visual lockup.

## Containing-field relationship

The asset will live inside a simple portfolio section below the current last Specialists chapter.

It must therefore be portable between chapter boundaries and **must not inherit a full environment from Index, Silk, or another Specialist**. Do not place Patch in the Index safehouse, Silk's world, a construction yard, a city street, or another scenic location.

Treat the image as an isolated editorial lockup with transparent surround and modest grounding shadows. The portfolio page owns the surrounding field and spacing.

The lockup should feel visually complete enough to be a deliberate ending beat, but smaller and quieter than a full chapter composition.

## Patch canon

Patch canon comes from the Adventures of Patch repository and is strict.

Canonical Patch is a friendly autonomous software-agent character in a clean editorial-vector style with thick rounded outlines and soft controlled shading. Preserve:

- compact rounded proportions;
- long-sleeved teal hoodie as the main body read;
- hood up and visibly framing the face;
- two slim dark antenna stalks with small rounded dark tips;
- off-white face panel;
- simple black oval eyes with tiny white shine highlights only;
- dark trousers;
- teal shoes with white soles;
- distinct crossbody bag with a dark strap crossing the torso from shoulder to opposite hip;
- dark bag flap / face with the white `>` mark clearly present when visible.

Patch must not become a generic robot, screen-face bot, toy mascot, glossy 3D character, plush character, human child, anime/chibi figure, or construction-worker caricature.

The Adventures canon explicitly permits small held props, tools, and costume / role gear when the story needs them **provided the gear does not overwrite Patch's hoodie/body/bag/face/antennae identity**. The hard hat is such role gear.

## Hard-hat treatment

The hard hat is bright construction yellow and clearly reads as an accessory placed **over the existing teal hood**, not as a replacement head shell or robot helmet.

Requirements:

- the teal hood remains visible around the off-white face panel;
- both canonical dark antennae remain present and legible;
- use one specific construction: a shallow yellow hard hat with two neat, minimal crown slots; both unchanged antenna stalks emerge cleanly through those slots, while the teal hood remains continuously visible beneath the brim and around the face panel;
- do not bend, remove, enlarge, recolour, duplicate, or redesign the antennae to make the hat work;
- no visor, face shield, chin guard, helmet faceplate, or sci-fi armour treatment;
- no logo or text on the hard hat.

The visual joke is simply “Patch has put a hard hat on because this bit is still being built.” Do not over-design it.

## Patch action and expression

Patch is **mid-task**, not posing as a boss, foreman, authority figure, or mascot.

Use a warm, competent, slightly pleased expression. The body language should say that the work is in hand and Patch expects to be back at it shortly.

Preferred action:

- Patch stands beside the sign in a relaxed three-quarter pose;
- one hand may rest on the sign edge / top support or gesture toward it;
- the other hand may hold a small clipboard, rolled plan, or remain naturally at Patch's side;
- keep the crossbody bag readable and physically coherent;
- do not let the sign or props hide Patch's face, antennae, bag identity, or main silhouette.

Avoid heroic construction poses, crossed-arm authority poses, thumbs-up stock-mascot poses, exaggerated comedy, or theatrical “ta-da” presentation.

## Sign

The sign is a simple freestanding temporary-work sign, sized as the second major element of the lockup after Patch.

It should have:

- a broad off-white / pale neutral face;
- a dark rounded border or simple practical frame;
- a sturdy, believable stand or two-leg support;
- subtle yellow / charcoal construction accents if useful;
- enough clean unbroken face area for later HTML copy.

Keep the sign face square to the viewer: an axis-aligned rectangular copy surface with no perspective taper, rotation, curved face, folded edge, or foreshortening. Perspective may appear only in the frame thickness, stand, feet, and ground shadow. The page must be able to place crisp rectangular HTML text over the face without transformation or perspective mapping.

**Generate the sign face blank.** Do not generate words or pseudo-text into the pixels.

The intended page-owned copy is:

> UNDER CONSTRUCTION
>
> Check back soon.

This text is context for sizing and visual hierarchy only. It will be rendered as HTML so it remains exact, accessible, responsive, and editable.

Leave generous safe space around that future text. Treat the central sign face as a clean copy-safe rectangle. Hazard tape, Patch's hands, shadows, props, scratches, highlights, bolts, and frame elements must not enter it. If Patch touches the sign, the hand belongs on the outer frame or support only.

## Cone and hazard tape

Use one ordinary orange traffic cone as a small grounding prop, not a second focal point.

Use one short run of graphic hazard / barrier tape as a compositional connector. Prefer simple yellow-and-charcoal diagonal striping with no printed words.

The tape may run between the sign support and a small post / cone, or sit behind the main subjects as a framing diagonal. It must not:

- cross Patch's face;
- cross the clean sign-copy area;
- wrap Patch up;
- dominate the lockup;
- imply danger, disaster, police cordon, crime scene, or aggressive exclusion.

The construction language is playful and temporary, not threatening.

## Composition

Primary family: `frame/surface`, directed viewing contract.

Supporting family: `object/body`.

Compose a single landscape lockup with a clear left-to-right read:

1. Patch;
2. the under-construction sign;
3. cone / tape as supporting punctuation.

Preferred arrangement:

- Patch occupies the left or left-centre of the lockup;
- sign occupies the right or right-centre;
- cone sits low near the sign / between the two major elements;
- tape creates one useful diagonal or boundary line without becoming a fence;
- modest soft ground shadows keep all objects on one believable plane;
- transparent negative space surrounds the group.

Keep the overall silhouette compact and readable. Avoid a long horizontal chain of tiny props.

The lockup should still read clearly when reduced substantially on the page.

## Attention path

First read: canonical Patch in the yellow hard hat.

Second read: the broad blank construction sign, obviously intended to carry the section message.

Third read: cone and tape completing the “temporary work in progress” gag.

Patch and sign should feel like one authored visual unit rather than a character placed next to unrelated clip-art props.

## Style, line, shading, and material

Use the approved Adventures of Patch visual language:

- clean editorial vector illustration;
- thick, smooth, rounded outlines;
- simplified readable shapes;
- soft controlled shading;
- friendly, purposeful tone;

The sign may have slight practical wear, but it should still feel tidy and cared for. This is an intentional temporary section, not a derelict building site.

## Output contract

- One candidate per generation call.
- One composed lockup asset, not a sheet and not multiple isolated deliverables.
- Request a transparent-background PNG. Genuine alpha is a hard acceptance requirement even when the generation surface exposes no structured transparency control.
- Request a landscape 3:2 working frame, targeting approximately `1536 × 1024`, with the visible lockup comfortably inside the alpha canvas. Provider-normalised pixel dimensions are acceptable only when the returned composition remains 3:2 and nothing is clipped; inspect the actual file rather than trusting the request.
- Keep enough transparent breathing room that the page can mount the whole lockup without accidental edge clipping.
- Do not bake a coloured page background into the asset.
- Do not crop Patch's antennae, hard hat, shoes, sign feet, cone, or tape endpoints accidentally.
- Keep the sign's blank copy face large enough to remain useful after responsive reduction.
- The returned composition must work as-delivered; do not rely on a corrective crop, redrawing, generative repair, or composite rebuild. A later deterministic resize or encoding conversion may create a delivery derivative without changing the composition.
- Inspect actual dimensions, aspect ratio, colour mode, alpha values, and transparent edge quality. PNG encoding alone does not prove transparency.
- If genuine usable alpha is absent, dimensions materially distort the requested frame, or a hard invariant fails, mark the candidate `generated but unresolved`. Do not select or promote it.
