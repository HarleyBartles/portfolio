## Goal

Turn the existing Agentic Learning Lab project note into a senior-level case
study about mentoring, curriculum architecture, learner safety, and practical
agentic engineering.

The page must prove more than the existence of teaching files. It must show
that Harley can sequence difficult technical ideas, design environments in
which another person can experiment safely, distinguish human authority from
agent capability, revise a model when evidence disproves it, and make the
result freely reusable by other learners and facilitators.

The governing thesis is:

> The learner is not the agent's hands. They learn to direct, inspect, verify,
> recover, and direct again.

The supporting proposition is:

> Agentic engineering is not reserved for programmers. The Learning Lab gives
> a learner durable mental models for using agents in whichever domain matters
> to them.

## Binding execution model

This binding, prospective execution model governs implementation,
continuation, or rework begun after 23 August 2026. It does not claim who
planned, implemented, reviewed, or accepted earlier work.

GPT-5.6 Sol is the sole main phase orchestrator. Sol reads the roadmap, this
approved phase specification, current repository truth, the portfolio design
policy, design-decision ledger, and relevant runbooks; writes the JIT
implementation plan; selects `/subagent-driven-development`; and maintains the
whole-plan view, task sequencing, integration, evidence, handoff readiness,
and completion drive.

Every subagent must use GPT-5.6 Terra. This includes implementation,
research, repair, task-review, re-review, and final-review subagents. Only the
main GPT-5.6 Sol orchestrator may create subagents: Terra workers cannot
delegate or create children. A Terra worker may propose decomposition or a
fresh-context review to Sol; Sol alone decides dispatch, role and reasoning
effort, sequencing, budget, concurrency, and reconciliation, and records that
decision in the plan or ledger. Keep the topology shallow: Sol -> Terra only;
Terra -> Terra descendants are prohibited. Generic escalation must not create a
Sol child: the Sol main agent narrows or replans the work and redispatches
Terra.

Before Terra begins material creative work, Sol records a phase-specific
creative-review brief in the JIT plan. Sol derives it from the approved phase
outcome, non-goals, protected defaults, design policy, decision ledger, and
current repository truth. The brief names the audience, intended response,
constraints and protected defaults, factual and privacy boundaries,
distinctive design intent, failure modes, observable acceptance signals, and
evidence surface.

The JIT plan records Sol's selected review lenses. Sol must use
`/writing-with-clarity` and the matching `/unslop-profiles` profile for
material prose, creative writing, documentation, plans, and handoffs, plus
the relevant artifact-specific skills and doctrine lenses. Model reputation or
an unsupported claim that Sol has better taste is never acceptance evidence.

Terra may draft creative work, but Sol personally reviews every material
creative output: public copy, creative writing, visual style, art direction,
hierarchy, imagery and capture framing, and interaction tone. Sol assesses
taste, humanness, restraint, specificity, and AI-slop risk against the £10k
portfolio bar.

Sol inspects the actual rendered or readable artifact, not Terra's
self-description, and records `pass` or `veto`, the artifact and evidence
reviewed, every criterion result, limitations, and unresolved human gates. A
veto becomes a bounded Terra revision brief naming the failed criterion or
emergent defect, observed evidence, intended effect, preserved constraints,
and re-review evidence. It constrains badness and preserves intent without
prescribing the creative answer or collapsing the result into formula.

The rubric is a floor and diagnostic aid, never an exhaustive formula or taste
scorecard; satisfying its listed criteria does not force a pass. Sol may veto
technically compliant work that is lifeless, generic, overwritten, derivative,
predictable, or off-tone, but must identify the artifact evidence, observed
defect, and intended effect. Unarticulated dislike is insufficient.

This process supports consistent, inspectable review and evidence-backed
decisions. It does not mechanically prove taste, humanness, originality, or
£10k quality. CI, profile conformance, or model identity cannot substitute for
Sol's review or a named Harley gate.

This creative gate precedes and does not replace any named Harley approval or
factual, privacy, custody, accessibility, deployed-proof, or protected-default
gate. Iteration stops only on a recorded pass or a genuine Harley-owned
decision.

> hard-earned agentic practice into something he could learn safely and use

> any learner or facilitator to adapt.

The final copy may tighten punctuation and rhythm without changing the

### Founding prompt

Quote this short fragment from the original prompt:

> “I'm going to teach my brother a few things about using agentic AI. I don't
> really have a learning plan or anything.”

Treat it as an honest origin artefact. Do not polish it into a fictional
requirements brief or mock it for being incomplete.

The visual and narrative payoff is the transformation from that partial goal
into a structured, inspectable curriculum. Like the Club DB artefact in the
Patch case study, origin work may be scrappy because its role is to show where
the project began, not to impersonate today's production standard.

### Public proposition

Do not use the absolute claim `I can teach anyone anything with agents`.

Use the defensible proposition:

> No programming background is required. The curriculum teaches transferable
> agentic-engineering judgment that a learner can apply to work in their own
> domain.

This communicates breadth without turning an unrun curriculum into a universal
outcome guarantee.

### Core learning loop

The principal behavioural loop is:

```text
learner directs
      ↓
agent performs useful work
      ↓
learner inspects, verifies, and questions
      ↓
agent explains observable actions and evidence
      ↓
learner redirects
```

The learner does not need to reproduce code, Git commands, scripts, database
operations, or implementation ceremony manually merely to be allowed to use
them.

They do need to understand the resulting state well enough to direct the
agent, inspect what actually happened, verify the outcome, question unfamiliar
machinery, and decide what happens next.

Do not describe agent explanation as privileged access to private
chain-of-thought. The learner needs observable actions, rationale, evidence,
assumptions, and state transitions.

### Useful models, deliberately refined

The curriculum does not front-load the final ontology of every agentic
concept. It gives a learner a model that is useful and substantially correct
for the current work, lets them use it, then introduces a later pressure that
earns a richer model.

Each course is a credible stopping point. No known materially false model is
carried across a course boundary merely because a later course could repair
it.

This is progressive disclosure with accountability, not withholding the
answer as theatre.

### The construction corrected the premise

The initial prompt leaned toward proving why local agents were superior to
cloud chat. The curriculum itself was initially built through cloud ChatGPT
using the GitHub connector.

That evidence forced a better position:

> Cloud, local, connected, and mixed environments are different access and
> capability surfaces. Choose according to the project and task rather than a
> universal hierarchy.

The page should present this as Harley revising the teaching model in response
to evidence, not as an embarrassing contradiction.

### The method built the method

Harley did not arrive with the complete curriculum architecture.

The approved authorship boundary is:

- Harley brought experience, learner knowledge, examples, constraints,
  judgment, corrections, preferences, and acceptance authority;
- agents proposed, drafted, reorganised, and persisted substantial curriculum
  implementation;
- Harley inspected, questioned, rejected weak work, redirected, and accepted
  what earned durable state; and
- the repository, history, PRs, tests, and current lab surfaces show what
  became real.

Use this line once:

> The method built the method: direct the agent, inspect the work, reject weak
> ideas, preserve useful ones, verify what became real, then instruct again.

Do not reduce the story to `AI wrote a course`, imply manual authorship of
every line, or use agent assistance as a substitute for evidence of curriculum
quality.

## Curriculum contract

### Course 1 — Agentic Engineering 101: Zero to Hero

Current range: Labs/Modules 1–10.

Learner outcome:

> I can direct, understand, provision, navigate, verify, and safely operate
> useful agent work.

Course 1 takes the learner from ordinary chat use to competent practical
agentic engineering and ends with a real learner-owned project.

Current module sequence:

1. From chatbot to worker.
2. Give the cloud agent the project.
3. The project has a home.
4. Repositories, save points, and safe breakage.
5. Model, harness, context, tools, and behaviour.
6. What does the model know?
7. Tools, operating knowledge, and domain provisioning.
8. What did we just create? Local work and connected systems.
9. Source of truth and verification.
10. Build a real agentic project.

At the design evidence baseline, Labs 1–7 are mature and 8–10 remain roadmap
modules.

Lab 10 is Course 1's synthesis and course boundary. It is not a detached
intermission.

### Course 2 — Advanced Agentic Engineering: Mastering Agents

Current range: Modules/Labs 11–15 plus 14A.

Learner outcome:

> I can deliberately design agent behaviour, workflow, context, delegation,
> evaluation, and autonomy.

Current module sequence:

11. Agent self-introspection and local review.
12. Autonomous human-in-the-loop workflows.
13. Specialist sub-agents and orchestration.
14. Harnesses, portability, and agent observability.
14A. The 20-Agent Bonfire and context transport.
15. Selective provisioning, context, and evaluation.

At the design evidence baseline, these remain roadmap modules rather than
mature labs.

The Bonfire is an economics, default-worker, orchestration, and context-
transport pressure test. It is not the shared-mutable-state concurrency lesson
owned by Course 3.

### Course 3 — Beyond the Agent: Engineering Agent Systems

Current range: Modules/Labs 16–18.

Learner outcome:

> I can design the wider system in which agents coordinate, act under trust
> and capability boundaries, work concurrently, integrate state, preserve
> provenance, and spend resources proportionately.

Current module sequence:

16. Trust boundaries and connected autonomy.
17. Concurrent agents and isolation.
18. Epilogue: show how this was built.

At the design evidence baseline, these remain roadmap modules rather than
mature labs.

The epilogue is a final worked system problem inside Course 3. It uses the
curriculum repository and learner fork as evidence surfaces and asks the
learner to reconstruct what the available history proves, infers, or cannot
recover.

### Cognitive grades, not credentials

The three outcomes are cognitive grades. They describe the unit of system the
learner is asked to reason about.

They are not:

- job titles;
- certification levels;
- guaranteed mastery;
- claims that a learner becomes a professional software engineer; or
- claims that a fixed number of hours produces an identical outcome for every
  learner.

Use `competent agentic engineer`, `advanced agentic engineer`, and
`agent-systems engineer` only inside the clearly labelled cognitive-grade
explanation.

## Module and lab distinction

### Module

A module is a durable curriculum roadmap unit. It defines the learning
pressure, dependencies, intended model change, and material that should
eventually become a runnable lab.

A module may be substantial and thoughtfully planned without being ready for
a learner.

### Mature lab

A mature lab is a module promoted into a complete runnable teaching
experience. Depending on the topic, it includes:

- an explicit learner outcome;
- facilitator rationale and setup;
- learner-facing cards revealed in sequence;
- a bounded mission, project, or conversational experiment;
- stable starting conditions;
- authority, permission, privacy, and recovery boundaries;
- expected observations without forcing a scripted model response;
- fallbacks for model variance and poor example fit;
- a reset path;
- deliberate `do not teach yet` boundaries; and
- a clean handoff into the next lab.

Do not describe maturity as mere file completeness.

### Promotion visual

Use this source order:

```text
roadmap module
      ↓
learning pressure and dependencies
      ↓
facilitator choreography
      ↓
learner cards and progressive disclosure
      ↓
bounded worker environment and safety controls
      ↓
runnable lab with recovery and handoff
```

The final stage is labelled `mature lab`, not `finished forever`. Mature labs
can still improve through future delivery and evidence.

Public proof:

- the learner inspects the accumulated change set rather than trusting prose;
- a plausible answer and a verified answer are different states of knowledge;

+ instructions and settings
+ context
+ tools and permissions
+ environment and state

## Safety and authority model

The laboratory is deliberately designed so the learner can make mistakes.

Use the source line:

> What is the blast radius, and do I have a recovery path?

The safety argument must include:

- the learner begins with a forked copy containing nothing precious;
- mission and project environments are scoped narrowly;
- facilitator choreography stays outside the worker's assigned root;
- instructions describe intended behaviour while permissions bound possible
  behaviour where the harness provides them;
- working state, committed state, pushed state, and external side effects are
  taught as different boundaries;
- recovery targets the smallest bad change rather than using broad destructive
  resets;
- agents do not silently publish, connect, or mutate consequential external
  state; and
- humans retain semantic authority, acceptance, and consequential gates.

Do not use fear-based security language or imply that Git recovers every
external consequence.

## Principal visual contracts

### Learning loop

The learning loop is the hero's principal visual.

Required nodes:

1. Direct.
2. Agent works.
3. Inspect.
4. Verify.
5. Question.
6. Explain observable work.
7. Redirect.

The final composition may group `inspect`, `verify`, and `question` as one
human-judgment stage while keeping all three words visible.

Human-owned nodes and agent-performed nodes must remain distinguishable
without colour alone.

The loop cannot imply that agent explanation is proof or that the agent accepts
its own work.

Use semantic HTML with decorative connectors hidden from assistive technology.
The accessible description presents the source order once.

### Curriculum atlas

The atlas shows all eighteen module titles once inside three course groups.

Requirements:

- keep `14A` attached to Course 2 and visually subordinate to 14 without
  hiding it;
- show a one-sentence outcome for each course;

### Module-to-lab promotion

Use an ordered semantic flow, not an opaque funnel.

Each stage has:

- a short label;
- the question it answers;
- one output; and
- a stop condition where useful.

Decorative arrows may disappear under reduced motion or narrow layouts without
changing meaning.

### Lab cutaway

The cutaway has three visible regions:

1. `Facilitator` — rationale, setup, observations, fallbacks, and deferments.
2. `Learner` — one revealed card, next action, questions, and judgment.
3. `Worker environment` — only the bounded mission/project state and its
   applicable instructions, tools, and permissions.

The visual must make this non-flow explicit:

```text
facilitator choreography
        ✕ not worker context

learner instruction
        → bounded worker environment
```

Do not imply that concealment is a security boundary when it is only workspace
scope. Where permissions provide enforcement, label that separately.

## Motion contract

Motion is supporting feedback, not an interactive lesson.

Allowed motion:

- a restrained first-view trace through the learning loop;
- a short course-to-course line progression when the atlas enters view; and
- small hover or focus emphasis on linked evidence.

Motion must not:

- gate content;
- change maturity state;
- reorder the curriculum;
- auto-advance course folios;
- create parallax between text and its label;
- loop indefinitely; or
- become necessary to understand a relationship.

Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, render the complete final state
immediately and disable non-essential transitions.

The implementation may choose a fully static composition if motion adds no
material clarity.
