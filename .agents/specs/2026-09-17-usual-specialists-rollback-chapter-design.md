# The Usual Specialists V2: Rollback Chapter Design

**Status:** Approved chapter direction; local composition requires further Harley design dialogue before JIT planning

**Parent page spec:** `2026-09-17-usual-specialists-visual-story-design.md`

**Chapter job:** Preserve recoverability when the chosen plan fails.

## Settled story contract

Klause has just committed the crew to a plan. Rollback's chapter immediately treats
that certainty as something that can fail.

Rollback provides Plans B, C, D through Z and the plan for when all the plans burn,
but his deeper job is not alphabetic contingency. He protects recoverability when
named plans stop being useful.

His key question remains:

`What's your Plan B?`

The chapter must make clear that this is the question Patch came to have asked, not
a gotcha proving Patch incompetent.

## Audience change

The reader should move from "we have a committed plan" to "failure no longer means
loss of the crew's ability to recover."

Silk creates chaos deliberately to test a route. Rollback absorbs chaos and keeps
recovery possible. That distinction is essential.

## Primary visual family and current direction

**Primary family:** encounter/sequence.

**Supporting families:** mass/stillness and counterfactual failure.

The environment should move more than Rollback. Systems fail, shutters move,
states change and containment reacts while Rollback remains the stable mass inside
the field.

Do not turn the chapter into a giant branching B-through-Z infographic.

## Safehouse territory

Rollback has taken the apartment basement because its concrete walls make it the
obvious place to run containment and recovery tests.

CRT/monitor language, heavy glass, practical containment infrastructure and active
simulations remain useful. The environment is industrial recovery space, not
military theatre.

## Settled beat material

The detailed composition is still open, but preserve these approved events:

1. Patch arrives carrying the post-Klause folder state while Rollback is already
   running unrelated tests.
2. Plan A or an equivalent active simulation visibly fails.
3. The environment changes aggressively; Rollback calmly contains it.
4. He hears Patch out and asks `What's your Plan B?`
5. The exchange reveals recoverability deeper than a finite stack of backups.
6. Rollback snatches his own dog tags from around his neck and tosses them to
   Patch.
7. He turns straight back to containment work. Business concluded.

## Explicitly removed beat

There is **no** CRT `I'M IN` assent state.

The monitor may identify test state, containment state or useful operational
information, but it must not duplicate the meaning already carried by the dog-tag
handoff.

## Folder continuity and Adventures pre-vis reference

Rollback inherits Index + Silk + Writ + Klause continuity.

The relevant Adventures of Patch stage-level continuity pre-vis is:

`workbench/issue_48_override_heist_style_framework_v0_3/style-sheets/heist_pitch_folder/04_rollback_joined.png`

Use it as reference for the physical dog-tag material language and how the tags can
hang from the folder/spine. Do not copy its historical stage number as story order:
that pre-vis puts Rollback before Writ, while V2 places Rollback after Klause.

The tags are the closing hero because they come directly from Rollback's own body.
The gesture is surprisingly personal; Rollback gives it no sentimental ceremony.

## Responsive survival requirement

Whatever composition is approved later must preserve the contrast between a
highly active failing environment and Rollback's calm physical stability.

Reduced motion must still show the before/after containment state without relying
on animation. At 320 CSS px and actual 200% zoom, the chapter still needs one
legible failure, one recovery response and one unambiguous dog-tag assent.

## Design questions that remain open

1. What is the dominant Rollback composition: one large catastrophe/containment
   field with subordinate beats, or a short sequence of test states?
2. How do we show recovery depth beyond Plan B without a branching infographic?
3. Which failure event is concrete enough to read immediately while remaining
   unrelated daily test work rather than the actual caper?
4. How does Rollback's stillness carry visual weight across wide and narrow
   layouts while the environment owns the state changes?
5. What exact camera/staging makes the dog-tag toss the closing hero while
   Rollback is already returning to the next containment problem?
6. How does the Klause-to-Rollback seam make the committed plan feel vulnerable
   without stealing Rollback's own first failure beat?

## Non-goals

- Do not restore CRT `I'M IN`.
- Do not turn Rollback into a soldier, weapons character or military set.
- Do not make Plans B-Z a giant branching diagram.
- Do not make Rollback create chaos in Silk's manner.
- Do not make the dog tags a decorative prop table object; they come off his neck.
- Do not commission final imagery before the dominant field and failure sequence
  are approved in design.

## Planning readiness

This specification is intentionally **not** ready for JIT implementation planning.
The role, basement, calm-vs-failure contrast, essential question, removed CRT beat
and dog-tag assent are settled. The composition is not. Resume brainstorming,
approve the failure/containment field, update this spec and pass spec-readiness
before writing Rollback's plan.
