## Goal

Use the homepage's first ten seconds to make Harley legible as a senior full-
stack engineer with an agentic-engineering edge, then give the visitor an
immediate route into useful evidence of how he thinks.

The homepage must feel dynamic and authored without sacrificing accessibility,
user preferences, reliability, or comprehension. Dynamic selection changes
which evidence receives emphasis. It must never determine whether the evidence
is available.

The reader should not primarily experience an attempt to sell Harley. The page
should read as a compact editorial account of how one engineer's judgement
applies across different domains. The professional action remains discoverable
and explicit, but proof leads and About retains the concentrated hiring
argument.

## Outcome

Phase 8 delivers:

- an editorial first viewport that answers both `who is this?` and `why should
  I inspect the work?`;
- an explicit senior full-stack proposition with agentic engineering as the
  differentiating edge rather than the entire identity;
- one session-stable project-or-essay doorway with a direct content CTA;
- category-balanced selection from four flagship projects and every eligible
  published essay;
- a complete, manually controlled four-project evidence deck;
- one low-hierarchy Patch fairytale interlude that is useful on the homepage;
- one art-directed essay feature and a route into the complete writing space;

- responsive, accessible, performant, project-native presentation;

  homepage defaults; and

## Binding execution model

This binding, prospective execution model governs implementation,
continuation, or rework begun after 23 August 2026. It does not claim who
planned, implemented, reviewed, or accepted earlier work.

GPT-5.6 Sol is the sole main phase orchestrator. Sol reads the roadmap, this
approved phase specification, current repository truth, the portfolio design
policy, design-decision ledger, and relevant runbooks; writes the JIT
implementation plan; selects `/subagent-driven-development`; and maintains the
whole-plan view, task sequencing, integration, evidence, handoff readiness,
and completion drive.

Every subagent must use GPT-5.6 Terra. This includes implementation,
research, repair, task-review, re-review, and final-review subagents. Only the
main GPT-5.6 Sol orchestrator may create subagents: Terra workers cannot
delegate or create children. A Terra worker may propose decomposition or a
fresh-context review to Sol; Sol alone decides dispatch, role and reasoning
effort, sequencing, budget, concurrency, and reconciliation, and records that
decision in the plan or ledger. Keep the topology shallow: Sol -> Terra only;
Terra -> Terra descendants are prohibited. Generic escalation must not create a
Sol child: the Sol main agent narrows or replans the work and redispatches
Terra.

Before Terra begins material creative work, Sol records a phase-specific
creative-review brief in the JIT plan. Sol derives it from the approved phase
outcome, non-goals, protected defaults, design policy, decision ledger, and
current repository truth. The brief names the audience, intended response,
constraints and protected defaults, factual and privacy boundaries,
distinctive design intent, failure modes, observable acceptance signals, and
evidence surface.

The JIT plan records Sol's selected review lenses. Sol must use
`/writing-with-clarity` and the matching `/unslop-profiles` profile for
material prose, creative writing, documentation, plans, and handoffs, plus
the relevant artifact-specific skills and doctrine lenses. Model reputation or
an unsupported claim that Sol has better taste is never acceptance evidence.

Terra may draft creative work, but Sol personally reviews every material
creative output: public copy, creative writing, visual style, art direction,
hierarchy, imagery and capture framing, and interaction tone. Sol assesses
taste, humanness, restraint, specificity, and AI-slop risk against the £10k
portfolio bar.

Sol inspects the actual rendered or readable artifact, not Terra's
self-description, and records `pass` or `veto`, the artifact and evidence
reviewed, every criterion result, limitations, and unresolved human gates. A
veto becomes a bounded Terra revision brief naming the failed criterion or
emergent defect, observed evidence, intended effect, preserved constraints,
and re-review evidence. It constrains badness and preserves intent without
prescribing the creative answer or collapsing the result into formula.

The rubric is a floor and diagnostic aid, never an exhaustive formula or taste
scorecard; satisfying its listed criteria does not force a pass. Sol may veto
technically compliant work that is lifeless, generic, overwritten, derivative,
predictable, or off-tone, but must identify the artifact evidence, observed
defect, and intended effect. Unarticulated dislike is insufficient.

This process supports consistent, inspectable review and evidence-backed
decisions. It does not mechanically prove taste, humanness, originality, or
£10k quality. CI, profile conformance, or model identity cannot substitute for
Sol's review or a named Harley gate.

This creative gate precedes and does not replace any named Harley approval or
factual, privacy, custody, accessibility, deployed-proof, or protected-default
gate. Iteration stops only on a recorded pass or a genuine Harley-owned
decision.

## Governing narrative

The homepage follows one stable cognitive sequence:

1. **Identity and invitation** — who Harley is, the professional level and
   breadth he offers, and one useful place to begin.
2. **Applied judgement** — four different project domains expose four distinct
   senior-level decisions.
3. **Memorable compression** — one Patch fairytale turns an agentic-engineering
   idea into a quickly consumable visual lesson.
4. **Articulated judgement** — one essay shows that the thinking can be
   explained, challenged, and carried elsewhere.
5. **Practical professional route** — experience, CV, availability, and
   contact remain easy to find without converting the page into a sales close.

The order does not change based on the selected doorway. Dynamic emphasis is
contained within the stable information architecture.

## First-viewport contract

### Professional proposition

The first viewport must make these facts legible in this order:

1. Harley Bartles;
2. senior full-stack engineer; and
3. an agentic-engineering edge expressed through the selected evidence and
   supporting thesis.

Do not make `agentic systems` appear to be the boundary of Harley's full-stack
practice. Do not repeat `senior` as an unsupported adjective throughout the
page. The masthead supplies context; the rest of the page supplies proof.

The exact final prose remains author-reviewed implementation copy. Objective
tests must not freeze a draft sentence when the required meaning can be tested
more robustly.

### Editorial front page

At desktop widths the masthead composes identity and one selected content
doorway as a single editorial front page. It must not read as two generic cards
or a conventional marketing hero followed by a carousel.

The identity region contains:

- the role proposition;
- Harley's name;
- a concise thesis that relates engineering judgement to the work; and
- a quiet route to About or professional details.

The selected doorway contains:

- a content-kind label;
- title;
- authored homepage proposition;
- appropriate status or editorial metadata;
- project-native or essay-native visual identity when available; and
- a direct, context-specific CTA into the canonical content route.

CTA language follows the content. Examples of intent are `Read the case study`
and `Follow the argument`, not a generic conversion label applied everywhere.

At representative desktop sizes, the role, selected title, and direct CTA are
all visible within the initial viewport. Achieve this with responsive
composition and type scale, not a brittle fixed-height container.

### Ten-second review

A hiring manager given ten seconds with the initial desktop surface should be
able to answer:

- who Harley is professionally;
- what distinguishes his practice;
- what evidence is being offered; and
- where to click to inspect it.

The test does not require the reviewer to remember every project, scan a
technology list, or reach the explicit contact route.

### Fast-to-break skepticism

Review the first viewport from the position of a hiring manager who has already
seen several polished agentic-engineering portfolios without finding convincing
software-engineering evidence. The stable identity frame must begin breaking
that skepticism before the selected doorway earns any additional credit.

The intended progression is:

1. within ten seconds, recognise a senior full-stack engineer with an agentic
   edge and encounter one concrete professional or technical signal;
2. within one deliberate click, reach substantive project, writing, or
   professional evidence rather than another positioning statement or the
   contact form alone; and
3. after one substantive evidence surface, find the vibe-coding interpretation
   untenable because the page exposes responsibility, decisions, trade-offs, or
   verification that generated polish cannot supply.

Random selection may vary the doorway, but it may not determine whether the
first viewport passes this test. The quiet professional route enters the top of
About or an equivalent evidence-bearing professional surface. Contact remains
available after the reader can inspect the case.

The professional signal must communicate consequence as well as scope. A role
label, ownership statement, or technology list alone is not the concrete signal
required here. Use the approved Phase 7A outcome account at a length the first
viewport can support, then route to the canonical About treatment for the full
stakes, change, outcome, and influence chain.

The direct content CTA should land where a project's question, design, proof,
cost, and current status are quickly legible. It must not force a skeptical
reader to reconstruct the case-study argument across several screens before
they know what kind of proof they are reading.

## Selection and session-state contract

### Category balance

Initial selection is category-balanced:

1. choose `project` or `writing` with equal probability; then
2. choose uniformly from eligible candidates in the chosen category.

This prevents future writing growth from crowding project evidence out of the
first viewport while keeping every eligible essay able to appear.

The category choice is editorial balance, not a claim that every individual
candidate has identical probability across the combined pool.

### Non-repeating order

The selector produces a complete non-repeating order for the current session.
The masthead's manual `show another` action advances through that order. It
must not choose the currently visible candidate again while another eligible
candidate remains.

The algorithm should be pure and accept an injected random source so tests can
prove category choice, candidate choice, and complete traversal without
probabilistic assertions.

### Session persistence

Persist only a versioned public selection receipt under a key such as:

```text
portfolio.home-evidence.v1
```

The receipt may contain:

- public candidate IDs in session order;
- the current doorway index; and
- the chosen fairytale ID.

It must not contain personal data, timestamps used for tracking, referral
information, or visitor behaviour beyond the UI state required to preserve the
experience.

The order and current selection survive internal navigation and Back within
the same browser session. A later browser session may receive a new order.

Storage access, parsing, and writes are wrapped in `try/catch`. Blocked,
unavailable, corrupt, stale-version, duplicated, unknown, or no-longer-eligible
values must not throw or leave a blank feature. Use an in-memory session for
the current mount when persistence is unavailable.

### Synchronous first state

The current catalogue is already bundled. Phase 8 must resolve a valid session
selection or fallback synchronously before committing the first masthead
feature state.

Do not:

- render the generic route loading page in place of the homepage;
- paint Marketplace and then visibly replace it after an effect runs;
- wait for image success before exposing the copy or CTA; or
- defer the role proposition until content selection completes.

The JIT plan may use a synchronous selector, lazy state initialiser, query
`initialData`, or another proportionate seam grounded in the then-current
repository. It must not introduce a runtime API for bundled metadata.

### Fallbacks

Stable fallbacks are:

- masthead doorway: Agent Asset Marketplace;
- writing feature: the vibe-coding essay;
- fairytale interlude: Goldilocks; and
- initial project lead when Marketplace occupies the masthead: the first
  eligible non-Marketplace project, initially Wild Bunch.

Fallback candidates are build-time requirements. Validation must fail when a
declared fallback is missing, unpublished, ineligible, or lacks its required
homepage contract.

When a category has no eligible content, select from the other category. When
the entire enhanced pool is invalid, render the complete Marketplace fallback
and retain working navigation. Do not modulo by an empty array, leave undefined
headings, or expose inactive controls.

## Manual doorway control

The masthead offers one restrained manual action to move to another candidate.
It is not a carousel toolbar and does not autoplay.

The control:

- is a native `button`;
- has an unambiguous accessible name;
- advances to a different candidate;
- updates the session receipt when storage is available;
- preserves focus on the activating control;
- announces the new title once through a polite status region; and
- swaps state immediately when reduced motion is requested.

Changing the doorway after initial arrival does not automatically reorder the
whole page or seize focus. Initial duplicate avoidance is an authored default;
repetition produced by deliberate later exploration is acceptable.

## Four-project evidence deck

### Purpose

The existing feature deck evolves into the applied-work section. Its frame is
not `a list of things Harley made`; it is the same engineering judgement under
four different constraints:

- governance and reusable capability boundaries;
- complex architecture that earns its cost;
- controlled creative production and technical communication; and
- mentoring, curriculum design, and capability transfer.

The section may express this relationship through its heading and composition,
but must not turn the four distinct proof responsibilities into four repetitive
claims of seniority.

### Complete availability

All four project titles, distinct propositions, statuses, and routes are
present in the section's semantic content on initial render.

One project receives the substantial visual lead. The other three remain
visible as typographic supporting entries. Interaction changes prominence, not
availability.

The initial visual lead is the first project in the session order that is not
the masthead project. When the masthead contains writing, use the leading
project from the session order.

### Manual controls

Preserve useful manual control with labelled Previous, Next, and a purposeful
random-choice action such as `Surprise me`. Exact visible wording is subject to
author review.

The controls:

- never autoplay;
- never hide the supporting entries;
- cannot select the current lead again when another project exists;
- retain focus;
- announce the new lead once;
- expose disabled or absent states honestly when the pool is too small; and
- do not change the masthead or writing feature after the visitor has begun
  interacting with the deck.

Do not import a general carousel dependency. The existing small feature-order
seam is the proportionate starting point and may be refactored into a clearer
home-evidence selection module.

## Responsive contract

### Source order

The meaningful source order is:

1. identity and role;
2. selected doorway copy and CTA;
3. selected doorway media;
4. applied-work section;
5. fairytale interlude;
6. writing feature; and
7. practical professional route.

Desktop visual placement must not create a contradictory keyboard or screen-

### Viewport behaviour

Review at 1440, 768, 390, and 320 CSS pixels and at 200% zoom.

At wide sizes:

- identity and doorway share the first viewport;
- role, selected title, and direct CTA are visible without scrolling; and
- media supports rather than displacing the semantic invitation.

At narrow sizes and high zoom:

- identity comes first;
- doorway title, proposition, and CTA precede optional media;
- the project lead and supporting entries stack in source order;
- the complete fairytale image retains its aspect ratio;
- controls wrap without horizontal overflow; and
- no content depends on side-by-side comparison to remain intelligible.

The page must remain usable at 320 CSS pixels without horizontal document
scroll. Do not use a fixed viewport height that clips content under browser
chrome, zoom, text resizing, or translated control labels.

## Accessibility contract

### Semantics and headings

Use one page `h1` for Harley's identity. Each major section has one meaningful
accessible heading. Feature titles use the next correct level within their
section.

Do not use tab semantics for a control that merely changes editorial
prominence. Do not use `aria-roledescription="carousel"` unless the implemented
interaction genuinely follows an accessible carousel contract and manual
testing demonstrates that it improves comprehension.

### Keyboard and focus

- every interactive element is reachable in meaningful order;
- native links perform navigation and native buttons change local state;
- focus remains visible against every supported background;
- changing a feature does not move focus or scroll position;
- hidden media or copy cannot retain focusable descendants; and
- skip navigation continues to reach the main content.

### Announcements

Doorway and project-lead changes use concise polite announcements such as `Now
featuring Wild Bunch`. Do not announce every decorative or metadata change.
Keep the status region present and empty when no update is required so assistive
technology receives one stable announcement surface.

### Images and equivalent meaning

Alt intent describes the visual evidence rather than repeating the adjacent
title. Decorative texture is hidden. Complex preview diagrams retain a nearby
text equivalent from their owning phase.

The fairytale's embedded words are not its only accessible content. The
takeaway and transcript route remain in real text.

### Reduced motion

No state change autoplays. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`:

- feature changes are immediate;
- no translate, scale, wipe, or crossfade transition remains perceptible;
- no content appears only after a reveal; and
- focus and announcements behave identically.

Without reduced motion, user-triggered transitions remain short and restrained
and communicate which item became prominent. They must not delay link or button
availability.

## Performance contract

Phase 8 remains within the repository's current bundle and image budgets unless
the implementation PR contains measured evidence and an approved budget
change.

The implementation must:

- use bundled manifest metadata rather than a runtime fetch;
- avoid an eager request for every candidate image;
- lazy-load below-fold media;
- preserve intrinsic dimensions or aspect ratios;
- avoid layout shift when the selected visual resolves;
- avoid a general animation, carousel, state, or image dependency;
- keep session-selection code small and tree-shakeable; and
- run the canonical build-budget check.

Randomness is not cryptographic and must not add a dependency. `Math.random`
behind an injected pure seam is sufficient.

## Failure and fallback behaviour

### Content metadata failure

Build validation rejects candidates missing required IDs, routes, propositions,
visual descriptions, statuses/datelines, or fallbacks. Do not defer a known
catalogue defect to runtime.

At runtime, filter invalid enhanced candidates and use the declared fallback.
The professional proposition and About route remain available even if all
enhanced evidence unexpectedly fails.

### Storage failure

Blocked or corrupt session storage silently degrades to a valid in-memory
experience. Do not show an error banner, consent prompt, or technical message
for optional UI persistence.

### Random-source failure

Guard non-finite or out-of-range injected values in the selection seam. Use the
stable fallback order rather than returning undefined state.

### Media failure

Copy, metadata, and CTA remain complete. The image region may collapse only
when the resulting composition is intentionally designed and produces no
layout instability; otherwise preserve a quiet typed field without a broken-
image icon or placeholder promise.

### Small pools

- one doorway candidate: render it and hide the another-feature control;
- one project: render it and hide project-change controls;
- no eligible fairytale: omit the interlude without leaving an empty heading;
- one essay: render it even when it also occupies the masthead, because a
  useful route is preferable to a blank section; and
- no writing: omit the feature and retain a working `/writing` route only when
  that route has useful published content.

These are defensive runtime states. The five-essay floor and four-project
launch contracts remain build-time acceptance requirements for the intended
release.

### Pre-hydration boundary

Phase 8 removes the homepage's avoidable post-hydration query wait and defines
a stable semantic fallback. It does not claim that static route documents now
contain the complete pre-hydration homepage. Phase 10 owns investigation and
finish of that wider direct-route first-paint boundary.
