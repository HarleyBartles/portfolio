# The Usual Specialists V2: Klause Chapter Design

**Status:** Approved chapter design; spec-readiness green; JIT plan waits for the actual Writ milestone output

**Parent page spec:** `2026-09-17-usual-specialists-visual-story-design.md`

**Chapter job:** Reduce authorised possibility to one executable decision.

## Settled story contract

Klause receives a reliable route and a lawful authority envelope. That still is
not a plan.

His job is reduction and commitment. He removes alternatives until the crew knows
what it is actually going to do.

Writ owns permission. Klause owns decision.

## Audience change

The reader should move from "there are viable, authorised ways to act" to "this is
the plan we are committing to."

The chapter itself should embody subtraction. It should become simpler as it
progresses.

## Primary visual family and approved direction

**Primary family:** stable-field staging.

**Supporting families:** rhythm through stillness/subtraction, plus selective
detail inserts.

Klause does not animate the chapter by moving through the room. The chapter moves
by deleting alternatives around him.

The governing visual sentence is:

**the page deletes itself**.

The composition begins sparse but plural, with one stable office field and only a
few subordinate fragments. As Klause rejects unnecessary possibility, those
supporting fragments disappear. The page becomes quieter, whiter and more singular
until one committed decision owns the field.

This is not a literal decision UI and not a collapsing dashboard. The visual
subtraction is editorial composition: fewer competing crops, fewer occupied
regions, more negative space and a clearer single axis.

The old wireframe's nine coverage cells are explicitly **not** current composition
authority. They correctly understood Klause's process as internal rather than a
room traversal, but they over-explained it as nine separate events. The approved
chapter uses five meaningful states instead.

## Safehouse territory and field treatment

Klause has converted one medium-sized apartment room into a clean, sparse,
deliberately uncluttered office because of course he has. Its austerity is
practical, not institutional.

The room itself is not important enough to become a visual subject. Preserve just
enough apartment and desk logic to keep the safehouse physically continuous, then
let the chapter compose itself primarily on an **IKEA-white working field**: the
most cost-efficient, least distracting workspace Klause could reasonably make.

The white field is not abstract placelessness. It is the apartment office reduced
to the parts Klause considers useful: desk geometry, chair relation, working
surface, aligned pen, binder/folder and bodies.

The field should therefore read as a decision environment built by somebody who
removes competing information rather than accumulating it.

## Motion and attention contract

Klause's efficiency is expressed through minimal movement.

He is already working when Patch arrives. The pen moves because Klause has work of
his own. Patch does most of the locomotion. Klause only moves enough to prove that
his state has changed:

- the pen stops;
- his eyes or attention shift;
- one small gesture may admit Patch into the chair;
- posture changes only as much as the decision requires;
- after the decision, the pen moves again.

The real motion belongs to **attention and subtraction**, not body travel. A tiny
gesture should carry disproportionate weight because the surrounding field is so
still.

## Approved five-state sequence

### 1. Already working

Begin with a sparse threshold/nameplate introduction that resolves quickly into
the stable office master field. Klause is doing his own work. His aligned pen is
moving. An empty chair is part of the geometry. Patch has not yet been admitted
into the composition.

### 2. The smallest possible interruption

The pen stops. Klause looks up.

If the chapter earns one tight detail insert, the stopped pen is the strongest
candidate because it stands for the entire transfer of attention. A minimal chair
indication may admit Patch without Klause getting up or turning the scene into a
welcoming performance.

### 3. Patch completes the geometry

Patch occupies the previously empty chair and completes the office relationship.
This is the chapter's maximum visual complexity while still remaining restrained:
the stable master field may coexist with a small number of subordinate readings.

Patch pitches. Klause listens and diagnoses the actual problem: there are too many
options. Klause is rejecting unnecessary possibilities while Patch talks.

### 4. Plural becomes singular

This is the Klause hero beat.

The subordinate fragments disappear. Occupied regions reduce. Negative space
expands. The master field becomes quieter and more dominant until there is only one
surviving visual proposition.

Klause himself does not need a dramatic pose. The page performs the reduction
around him. By the end of the beat, restoring the rejected material should feel
like breaking the composition. The decision is visually irreversible before the
assent mark appears.

### 5. Consequence, then attention withdrawn

Only after the decision exists does the recruitment folder need to come forward.
The folder's warmer manila colour and the produced **red `K` impression** become
the strongest colour event in the otherwise office-navy / teal / IKEA-white field.
The assent is the visible consequence of the now-singular decision, not a rubber-
stamp performance.

Patch leaves carrying that decision state. Klause's pen is already moving again
and his attention has returned to the work Patch interrupted.

The closing state echoes the opening condition: same worker, same desk, same work;
the interruption has simply been processed.

## Colour and attention hierarchy

Colour has a causal role rather than a decorative one:

- **Klause:** restrained office navy / slate blue;
- **Patch:** teal;
- **working field:** IKEA white and sparse neutrals;
- **final decision object:** warm manila folder with a red `K` impression.

Early in the chapter, navy and teal establish the two participants against the
quiet working field. As the page deletes its subordinate material, white gains
more compositional territory. At the final decision beat, the warmer folder and
red `K` mark break that restraint and pull the singular decision forward.

Colour must not be the only carrier of the result. Isolation, scale, reduced
competition and the changed folder state must make the decision readable without
colour alone.

## Folder continuity and Adventures pre-vis reference

Klause inherits Index + Silk + Writ continuity.

The relevant Adventures of Patch stage-level continuity pre-vis is:

Use it as reference for the physical language of the produced `K` assent mark and
its relationship to the accumulated folder. It is not a production continuity
master and its surrounding historical sequence is subordinate to V2 order.

The current chapter design deliberately sharpens that older material reference:
the final produced `K` impression is red so that the assent becomes the chapter's
single strongest decision-colour event. Do not copy a stale pre-vis ink colour over
this approved local rule.

The important object is the resulting mark on the folder, not a rubber-stamp tool,
ink pad or theatrical stamping performance.

## Assent and exit contract

Once the decision exists, the conversation is effectively over.

The produced `K` mark is the narrative answer. Klause immediately returning to the
work Patch interrupted is the character answer.

At this local closing beat the folder may become the strongest coloured object and
the immediate first read because it now carries the decision consequence. It must
not receive the completed-folder hero treatment reserved for the final page close.

He should not celebrate, explain the decision again or wait for Patch to leave.

## Copy and medium

Klause earns restrained copy through the nameplate, decision material and one
editorial story card.

Do not use authority language such as `Nothing moves unless Klause permits it` as
the chapter proposition. That belongs to Writ's domain. Klause's copy should deal
with choice, commitment, rejection and decision.

## Responsive survival requirement

Whatever composition is approved must preserve the chapter's decreasing
complexity. A narrow layout must not turn the reduction story back into a stack of
equally weighted cards.

At 320 CSS px, actual 200% zoom and reduced motion, the reader must still perceive:

- entry into a sparse decision space;
- competing possibility early;
- removal/rejection rather than accumulation;
- one final decision; and
- the `K` assent state carried into Rollback.

The desktop master-field arrangement is not itself sacred. At narrow widths the
chapter may use fewer or larger supporting fragments, but it must still get
visibly simpler rather than producing a long stack of equal-weight coverage.

The temporal/compositional invariant is:

**already working → tiny interruption → Patch completes the geometry → the page
deletes alternatives → one colour-led decision remains → Klause resumes work**.

Reduced motion must not weaken the chapter because motion is not the primary
carrier of meaning. Stillness, subtraction, changed attention, negative space and
the final folder state carry the story.

## Approved composition invariants

- One stable office master field carries the chapter.
- Klause remains desk-bound and physically economical.
- Patch supplies most locomotion by entering and occupying the missing chair.
- The stopped pen is the preferred detail-level sign of transferred attention.
- Early subordinate fragments are few and functional; they are progressively
  removed rather than multiplied.
- The chapter reaches its hero beat when plural composition becomes singular.
- IKEA white expands as alternatives disappear.
- The final folder state is the strongest colour event: warm manila plus red `K`.
- The `K` is a produced impression, not stamp-tool theatre.
- Klause has resumed his own work before Patch's departure becomes a scene.
- The chapter hands Rollback one committed decision to stress, not a menu of live
  alternatives.

## Non-goals

- Do not make Klause grant authority.
- Do not make Klause select Silk's route during recruitment.
- Do not restore the nine-panel wireframe by default.
- Do not use dashboards, route cards or decision-tree UI as a shortcut for
  decision discipline.
- Do not turn the disappearing composition into a software animation gimmick,
  collapsing-card UI or literal option counter.
- Do not make the apartment room a scenic hero; Klause's working field is allowed
  to reduce the room almost entirely to IKEA white and essential desk geometry.
- Do not make Klause walk the room or use unnecessary body motion to create energy.
- Do not keep all early fragments alive on narrow widths by stacking them.
- Do not let office navy, Patch teal or decorative accents compete with the final
  manila-folder / red-`K` decision event.
- Do not make the stamp tool the hero.
- Do not commission a fixed four-image roster before the live design proves which
  states actually require imagery.

## Planning readiness

The chapter design is approved and the specification is ready to hand to a planner
without asking that planner to invent the story role, five-state sequence, motion
logic, master-field relationship, subtraction grammar, colour hierarchy, assent or
exit state.

The remaining uncertainty belongs to the later live wireframe and implementation
stage: exact fragment count/geometry at each breakpoint, exact crop choices, exact
React/styled-components structure and which approved beats need newly commissioned
imagery.

Do **not** write Klause's JIT implementation plan yet. The roadmap is sequential,
and Klause's plan must consume the actual Writ milestone output and transition seam
rather than a hypothetical predecessor state.
