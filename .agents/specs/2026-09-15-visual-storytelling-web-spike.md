# Visual Storytelling Through Creative Frame Composition: Web Spike

**Status:** Research baseline for the visual-storytelling skills epic

**Researched:** 2026-09-15

## Research question

What can image-generation briefing and authored screen composition borrow from
photography, cinematography, comics, editorial design, theatre, lighting, anime,
and games without collapsing those fields into surface style?

The answer is a story-first composition system. The transferable material is not
the appearance of a comic, film, magazine, stage, anime production, or retro game.
It is the deliberate control of:

- what is visible, withheld, or implied;
- what is noticed first, next, and last;
- where the audience believes they stand;
- how subjects relate across depth, distance, boundary, and time;
- when the audience reads, pauses, searches, anticipates, discovers, or acts;
- how the composition changes their knowledge, feeling, orientation, or agency.

The core test for every technique is therefore:

> What story job does this choice perform here, and what would be lost if the
> choice were removed or replaced?

## The big picture

### Composition exists at nested scales

The research supports four nested scales:

```text
object -> frame -> complete field -> sequence
```

- **Object:** a face, hand, prop, building, tile, word, icon, or patch of light.
- **Frame:** one bounded view: photograph, shot, panel, viewport, inset, or screen
  region.
- **Complete field:** the whole image, page, spread, stage picture, screen, town,
  or navigable scene in which frames and objects coexist.
- **Sequence:** change across panels, cuts, pages, scroll, cues, animation, player
  movement, state transitions, or revisitation.

A single frame may carry an entire story. The same frame may also be subordinate to
a page, spread, route, scene, or world that carries a larger story. The skill must
always test both local success and contribution to the containing field.

### The audience contract changes the composition

- **Directed:** the author controls the view and order. Crop, focus, reveal, and
  timing may be precise.
- **Guided:** the audience controls pace, but the main route is authored. Hierarchy,
  rhythm, thresholds, and responsive survival matter more than exact timing.
- **Exploratory:** the participant controls viewpoint, route, order, or state. One
  photogenic angle is insufficient; landmarks, paths, gradients, repeated cues, and
  compositions from likely observation points must work together.

This distinction is essential when translating cinema or comics to a webpage, or a
still composition to a tile-based game town.

### Story beats precede visual techniques

Before choosing a technique, identify the beat:

- establish a place or relationship;
- orient or disorient;
- reveal, conceal, foreshadow, or surprise;
- isolate, connect, compare, or oppose;
- compress time or make time felt;
- transfer attention or point of view;
- turn an object or detail into evidence;
- show consequence, reaction, or changed state;
- give agency, invite discovery, or close a route.

Techniques are selected because they perform the beat, not because they are
canonical, cinematic, editorial, anime-like, or retro.

## Photography: selection, exclusion, and the decisive field

Photography contributes the strongest discipline of deciding what the frame admits
and excludes. Composition is not arranging everything pleasantly; it is committing
to a subject, viewpoint, distance, boundary, and moment.

Durable findings:

- **Crop is narrative.** A tight crop removes context and increases attention,
  intimacy, pressure, tactility, or ambiguity. A wide frame makes environment,
  scale, isolation, or relationship available. Background should remain only when
  it carries story.
- **Off-frame space remains active.** Gaze direction, motion, truncation, shadow,
  and leading vectors make the audience infer what lies beyond the boundary.
- **Point of view changes meaning.** Height, distance, angle, lens behaviour, and
  physical proximity determine power, vulnerability, observation, participation,
  and distortion.
- **Frame-within-frame is not merely decoration.** Doors, windows, bodies, foliage,
  shadow, architecture, or reflections can isolate, observe, confine, reveal,
  authenticate a viewpoint, or move the audience through depth.
- **Foreground elements create entry and depth.** A partial foreground can operate
  as repoussoir, viewpoint evidence, obstruction, surveillance, intimacy, or scale.
- **Leading lines are attention vectors.** Physical lines, light, shadow, colour
  edges, gesture, and gaze can route the eye. Their endpoint and escape direction
  matter more than their presence.
- **Focus is a hierarchy control.** Shallow focus isolates; deep focus makes
  relationships available; selective focus can conceal or destabilise. Blur is not
  an automatic synonym for quality.
- **Negative space has a job.** It may hold direction of travel, absence, waiting,
  vulnerability, copy, environmental pressure, or an implied unseen subject.
- **Close detail works through synecdoche.** Hands, eyes, worn surfaces, tools,
  documents, and traces can stand for labour, intent, emotion, identity, or prior
  action. A close crop needs an evidence-bearing detail, not merely proximity.
- **Rules are hypotheses.** Thirds, symmetry, centred framing, triangles, and other
  schemes are starting structures. Story purpose decides whether balance, imbalance,
  stability, or edge pressure is right.

Implication for the skill: it must route by narrative consequence of crop,
viewpoint, boundary, focus, and negative space, not teach a generic checklist of
composition rules.

## Cinematography: time, causality, focus, and the speaking camera

Cinematography adds duration, changing attention, blocking, continuity, and the
relationship between adjacent frames. AFI and ASC sources repeatedly frame camera,
lens, light, colour, movement, inclusion, and focus as story language rather than
technical garnish.

Durable findings:

- **Every shot has a grammatical function.** Camera placement determines who owns
  the view, which relationships are legible, and whether the audience observes,
  identifies, judges, discovers, or anticipates.
- **Shot scale changes narrative distance.** Wide shots establish spatial relations;
  mediums stage exchange and behaviour; close-ups privilege reaction or thought;
  inserts make objects and details causally important.
- **A detail shot must be earned by consequence.** Hands, eyes, controls, documents,
  wounds, or objects become powerful when they alter interpretation, action, or
  suspense. Otherwise they are ornamental coverage.
- **Depth can carry simultaneous story.** Deep-focus staging lets foreground,
  middle-ground, and background actions coexist and asks the audience to discover
  relationships. It increases agency but risks hierarchy collapse.
- **Focus can transfer story.** Rack focus changes the active subject without a cut.
  Split-field focus binds separated depth planes, often making cause and effect or
  observer and observed legible in one frame.
- **Foreground framing produces subjectivity.** Shooting past bodies, barriers,
  glass, doorways, or objects can establish witness, concealment, confinement, or
  divided knowledge.
- **Blocking is composition through time.** Movement toward, away, across, above,
  behind, or between others externalises relationship and subtext.
- **Single-shot composition can replace coverage.** A frame can combine close-up,
  insert, reaction, and environmental relation when their simultaneous presence is
  the point.
- **Camera motion requires a story verb.** Reveal, pursue, retreat, search, detach,
  destabilise, or transfer are meaningful; movement whose only justification is
  energy is not.
- **Aspect ratio is an authored boundary.** The intended frame must be preserved
  through production and presentation rather than guessed downstream.
- **A visual arc matters across the whole work.** Repeated framing, altered distance,
  changed light, or reversed blocking can show transformation more powerfully than
  isolated beautiful shots.

Implication for the skill: it needs frame-level direction and sequence-level
direction. It must ask what changes within the frame, between frames, and by the end
of the visual arc.

## Comics: page-and-panel simultaneity

Comics make the nested-scale problem unavoidable: readers encounter a panel as a
moment and the page as a visible field of relationships. They also control their
own pace, can look ahead, and infer action across gutters.

Durable findings:

- **A panel selects a moment; the gutter delegates change.** The audience performs
  closure between states. The omitted event can be more active than an illustrated
  transition.
- **Panel size controls emphasis and experienced duration.** Repeated small panels
  can accelerate or prolong through accumulation; a large panel can arrest, reveal,
  establish, or make the reader dwell.
- **The page is a super-composition.** Panel shapes, gutters, balloons, figures,
  vectors, tonal masses, and empty space create an entry point and reading path.
- **A background can become the field for many panels.** Insets over a large matte,
  splash, map, landscape, or environmental image allow the whole page to establish
  place or emotional state while subordinate panels deliver actions, details,
  reactions, or parallel threads.
- **Insets change voice and scale.** They can magnify evidence, interrupt time,
  supply memory, show simultaneity, or place a subjective fragment inside an
  objective field.
- **Polyptychs hold a stable frame while time changes.** Repeating the same setting
  with altered subjects or states makes absence, routine, duration, or transformation
  visible.
- **Continuous narrative uses one field for several moments.** Repeated characters
  across a shared environment can turn space into sequence.
- **Reaction is part of action.** A consequence or witness panel often carries the
  meaning that spectacle alone cannot.
- **Text is a spatial actor.** Captions, balloons, sound effects, and silence occupy
  weight, direct flow, and alter timing; they cannot be pasted onto a finished image
  without affecting composition.
- **Page turns and boundaries create reveal.** On screens, the analogue may be a
  scroll threshold, state change, viewport entry, or deliberate interaction.

Implication for the skill: frame and page direction must be separate output
contracts. “Make it comic-like” is never sufficient; the agent must state what the
panel/page relationship does for the story.

## Editorial and magazine design: pace, voices, and interruption

Editorial design contributes a grammar for combining long-form reading, images,
captions, side material, evidence, and variation across a whole publication.

Durable findings:

- **Editorial decisions precede templates.** Priority, purpose, content type, and
  reading mode are more durable than a chosen desktop arrangement.
- **A grid is infrastructure, not a visible style.** Compound grids create family
  resemblance while allowing radically different compositions. Empty modules are
  active; filling every slot destroys rhythm.
- **Scale controls pace.** Repeated small units accelerate scanning; large spatial
  zones slow the reader and signal consequence.
- **Consistency and disruption work together.** A stable underlying system makes a
  full-bleed image, displaced headline, dense text field, blank spread, marginal
  note, or abrupt colour change meaningful.
- **Different editorial voices need spatial identities.** Main narrative,
  annotation, evidence, chronology, quotation, and aside may differ in placement,
  type, colour, or rhythm while still belonging to one composition.
- **Margins can carry relationships.** References, thumbnails, captions, and notes
  can make reading relational instead of merely linear.
- **A spread is not two independent pages.** The fold, centre, edge, and cross-spread
  vectors shape a single field. The web translation is a complete viewport or
  authored scroll passage, not a literal imitation of paper.
- **Responsive translation should preserve emphasis, not coordinates.** Store the
  editorial decision—dominant image, interruption, supporting evidence, pace—not a
  brittle desktop arrangement.
- **Typography is image and tempo as well as text.** But typographic spectacle must
  serve the article's voice and reading task, not compete with it.

Implication for the skill: full-page composition needs story roles, dominant and
subordinate fields, density rhythm, interruption, and survival requirements. CSS
grid recipes and breakpoint implementation remain downstream.

## Theatre staging: relationship made spatial

Theatre contributes live picturisation: bodies, objects, levels, distance, gaze,
entrances, and audience relationship produce meaning in a shared field.

Durable findings:

- **Blocking externalises subtext.** Approach, withdrawal, crossing, touch,
  obstruction, turning away, and occupying another's territory can contradict or
  intensify spoken content.
- **Proximity is semantic.** Intimacy, alliance, threat, exclusion, status, and
  loneliness can be composed through distance and grouping.
- **Levels and planes establish power and legibility.** Upstage/downstage,
  high/low, clustered/isolated, open/occluded choices shape the stage picture.
- **Tableaux crystallise a relationship.** A held arrangement can state the social
  or emotional truth of a scene before anything moves.
- **Entrances and exits redraw the composition.** A new body changes balance,
  attention, routes, and implied future action.
- **Audience relationship changes authorship.** Proscenium contains and frames;
  thrust and in-the-round expose multiple viewpoints; promenade makes route and
  discovery part of the composition; direct address collapses observer distance.
- **Cues coordinate disciplines.** Light, sound, motion, scenery, and performance
  change together at dramatic thresholds.
- **Poetic space can outrank literal realism.** A few deliberately placed elements
  may carry stronger relationships than a fully dressed but semantically neutral set.

Implication for the skill: page and game composition should treat content blocks,
figures, UI, scenery, and empty zones as staged relationships. Theatre should not be
translated as curtains, spotlights, or proscenium decoration.

## Lighting: selective visibility and temporal composition

Theatre and cinematography sources converge on five useful objectives: visibility,
revelation of form, composition/focus, mood, and information. The crucial refinement
is **selective visibility**: the author chooses what can be read, when, and how.

Durable findings:

- **Light reveals and withholds information.** Darkness is not unused space; it can
  conceal, delay, simplify, threaten, protect, or make the audience search.
- **Direction reveals form.** Front light clarifies; side light models texture and
  conflict; back light separates silhouette and depth; low or high angles alter
  naturalism and power.
- **Quality changes emotional and material reading.** Hard light defines edges and
  shadows; soft light joins forms and reduces local contrast.
- **Falloff composes depth.** Brightness relationships separate planes, isolate an
  acting area, or connect several regions into one state.
- **Colour has roles, not adjectives.** Establish base world, subject separation,
  motivated sources, emotional pressure, state change, and exceptions. “Cinematic
  teal and orange” is not direction.
- **Motivation creates coherence.** Even stylised light benefits from an intelligible
  source, rule, or dramatic reason.
- **Light can move the story without moving objects.** A cue can transfer attention,
  disclose a region, alter time/place, signal a changed state, or prepare an entrance.
- **Contrast must survive its medium.** In UI this includes text legibility, state
  distinction, colour independence, and forced-colour/reduced-motion behaviour.

Web translation: lighting becomes luminance hierarchy, contrast fields, palette
roles, selective revelation, layered translucency, shadow, focus states, and timed
or interaction-driven cues. It is not merely dark mode or a warm colour palette.

Image-generation translation: briefs must name source, direction, quality,
intensity, falloff, shadow behaviour, motivation, affected planes, palette role, and
the story information revealed or withheld.

## Anime: layout, decisive poses, selective motion, and compositing

Official Japanese studio material describes storyboards as time-and-screen design,
layout as the blueprint for each shot, key animation as decisive motion drawings,
and compositing as the assembly of coloured animation, backgrounds, CG, light, and
camera treatment. Studio Ghibli describes layout as the single-sheet design for
background/character relationship, motion, camera work, speed, and photographic
processing—an important model for a unified visual brief.

Durable findings:

- **Layout is the integrated shot contract.** Camera, staging, background,
  character placement, movement, effects, and processing are decided together
  before finish.
- **Key poses carry intention.** The most informative silhouette, line of action,
  contact, anticipation, impact, and aftermath matter more than evenly animating
  everything.
- **Limited animation is allocation, not deficiency.** Hold what can remain still;
  spend motion where it changes meaning. Move a layer, camera, light, effect, mouth,
  hand, or environmental element selectively.
- **Stillness makes motion legible.** A held frame or environmental interval can
  create anticipation, aftermath, contemplation, scale, or lived-in time.
- **Backgrounds may act.** Weather, crowds, signs, machinery, light, and atmospheric
  layers can carry time, pressure, or story while characters remain still.
- **Compositing unifies layers.** Character, background, CG, effects, focus, colour,
  light, and camera treatment must appear to belong to one world.
- **Graphic deformation is event-specific.** Impact frames, speed abstraction,
  perspective exaggeration, or palette rupture should mark force or subjective
  intensity, not function as an all-purpose anime filter.
- **Consistency is production-wide.** Layout is valuable partly because it aligns
  many contributors around the same spatial and temporal intent.

Implication for the skill: borrow anime's production logic, not a studio's style.
The direction artifact should be layout-like: one coherent specification of frame,
layers, action, light, timing, and finish. Motion should be budgeted by story value.

## Modern game design: authored attention under player agency

Games change the audience from viewer to participant. The composition must support
orientation, choice, action, feedback, and return, often across many possible
viewpoints.

Durable findings:

- **Compose at object, viewport, scene, route, and world scales.** A good screenshot
  does not prove a good navigable composition.
- **Observation points are designed.** Entrances, funnels, thresholds, turns,
  overlooks, spawn positions, and pauses can present important compositions without
  stealing agency.
- **Landmarks need a support hierarchy.** Surrounding scale, silhouette, colour,
  light, motion, terrain, paths, and lesser forms relay attention toward the landmark.
- **Guidance is multimodal.** Leading lines, path width, material change, lighting,
  contrast, motion, sound, NPC gaze, repeated motifs, and UI can agree without each
  becoming an explicit arrow.
- **Player freedom changes balance.** Directed scenes may support one dominant view;
  exploratory spaces need robust silhouettes and cues from several approaches.
- **Environmental storytelling presents evidence.** Props, wear, damage, repair,
  occupation, traces, text, systems, and changed state invite inference. It must not
  invent unsupported canon merely to fill space.
- **Systems can tell stories dynamically.** The environment's reaction to player
  action can carry consequence more powerfully than a static tableau.
- **Pacing needs compression and release.** Dense action, narrow paths, calm
  traversal, vistas, social nodes, and returns make each other meaningful.
- **Motion is an attention resource.** A single moving element can make a still
  landmark discoverable; ubiquitous motion destroys that advantage.
- **Interface and world form one attention economy.** HUD, DOM controls, overlays,
  playfield, and environmental cues must not issue contradictory priorities.

### Tile-based towns and hubs

Tile systems intensify repetition. Authorship comes from hierarchy and controlled
exception rather than disguising the grid at all costs:

- roads, edges, junctions, thresholds, and sightlines establish large-scale flow;
- landmark silhouettes and footprint differences create district identity;
- repeated tile families establish a norm; meaningful exceptions signal status,
  history, prosperity, danger, or destination;
- prop density and wear can tell social/economic state, but noise must not erase
  route legibility;
- arrival, discovery, interaction, return, and departure each need a readable
  composition;
- deterministic variation should preserve authored hierarchy and seams;
- changed-world states should alter the field coherently, not merely swap isolated
  decorations.

Implication for Wild Bunch: direct the 10x10 town as a complete story field while
testing likely viewport compositions and preserving its existing camera, road,
view, density, seed, seam, and surrounding DOM-navigation contracts.

## Classic games and Amiga-era constraint invention

The Amiga hardware manuals make the causal chain explicit: colour depended on a
small table of registers; pixel values selected entries through bitplanes; the
Copper could change registers during display. Deluxe Paint exposed palette ranges
and cycling as authoring tools. Mark Ferrari's later GDC account demonstrates how
palette shifting can create complex background movement without frame animation.

Durable findings:

- **A palette is a role system.** Colours are shared resources assigned to world,
  subject, interface, depth, highlight, danger, animation, and state—not chosen
  independently for each object.
- **Bitplanes encourage separable meaning layers.** Modern work can borrow the idea
  of independently controllable semantic planes without pretending to reproduce the
  hardware.
- **Palette swapping can express state cheaply and coherently.** Time of day,
  allegiance, prosperity, damage, alert, weather, or location can change through a
  controlled remapping rather than repainting every asset.
- **Colour cycling is spatial animation.** Paint contiguous palette-index patterns
  so changing register values creates water, fire, shimmer, machinery, magic, or
  ambient flow without changing geometry.
- **Cycling requires semantic isolation.** If one cycling range is reused across
  unrelated objects, every occurrence animates. The palette plan is therefore part
  of composition and asset architecture.
- **Copper-style changes show that one frame can contain zones with different visual
  rules.** The transferable principle is scanline/region-specific state, not raster
  bars as decoration.
- **Constraint creates hierarchy.** Limited colours, sprites, memory, and tile sets
  force silhouette, reuse, contrast, and exception to carry more meaning.
- **Retro authenticity is causal.** Pixel size, limited palettes, cycling, dithering,
  tiles, parallax, and sprite limits should arise from an intended behaviour or
  production constraint. Applying them as nostalgia texture produces imitation
  without the clarity of the original systems.

Implication for the skill: include palette roles, tile repetition/exception,
parallax layers, sprite silhouette, palette swap, colour cycling, and regional
display logic. Keep hardware implementation in game-technology skills.

## Cross-disciplinary technique routing

| Story need | Primary technique family | Useful supporting fields | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Isolate a subject | tight crop, shallow focus, selective light | photography, cinema, theatre | arbitrary blur or empty background |
| Make detail carry the whole | insert, extreme close-up, synecdoche | cinema, comics, photography | fetish detail with no consequence |
| Show simultaneous cause and effect | deep staging, split focus, layered tableau | cinema, theatre, games | all planes competing equally |
| Create observation or confinement | frame-within-frame, occlusion, barrier, off-frame pressure | photography, cinema, theatre | decorative doorway framing |
| Establish place and deliver incidents | matte/super-panel with insets | comics, editorial, games | floating cards over wallpaper |
| Control a full-page story | dominant field, subordinate voices, density rhythm, interruption | editorial, comics, theatre | hero/cards/CTA template |
| Make relationship spatial | blocking, proximity, level, grouping, gaze | theatre, cinema, comics | positions chosen only for balance |
| Transfer attention over time | focus shift, lighting cue, movement, scroll/state threshold | cinema, lighting, anime | motion without semantic change |
| Make an impact readable | anticipation, decisive pose, deformation, aftermath | anime, comics, games | constant maximum intensity |
| Invite discovery without confusion | landmarks, relay hierarchy, paths, observation points | games, theatre, photography | one perfect screenshot |
| Show history through place | evidence tableau, wear, traces, system reaction | games, theatre, photography | invented lore or prop clutter |
| Animate ambience economically | selective layer motion, parallax, palette cycling | anime, classic games, theatre lighting | everything moving |
| Show changed state coherently | palette roles, repeated framing, spatial exception | games, cinema, editorial | unrelated local restyling |

## What belongs in `directing-visual-stories`

The creative-direction skill should own:

- story beat, intended audience change, and evidence boundary;
- object/frame/complete-field/sequence classification;
- directed/guided/exploratory viewing contract;
- crop, scale, viewpoint, perspective, aspect and boundary;
- foreground/middle-ground/background staging and occlusion;
- focus strategy and attention order;
- subject hierarchy, visual weight, vectors, negative and off-frame space;
- lighting intent, palette roles, selective visibility, and state cues;
- stillness, motion, transition, reaction, and sequence rhythm;
- page/spread/super-panel/inset/polyptych relationships;
- blocking, proximity, levels, entrances, and audience relationship;
- layout/key-pose/selective-motion/compositing intent;
- landmarks, routes, observation points, environmental evidence, and agency;
- tiles, repetition/exception, palette swaps, parallax, and colour-cycling intent;
- a rationale and avoid list tied to story function;
- survival requirements for downstream responsive, accessible, or interactive work.

It should route to focused references by story problem, select one primary technique
family and at most two supporting families, and produce a frame, page, or world
direction contract.

## What belongs in `generating-images`

The image-execution skill should own:

- checking whether creative direction is complete enough to execute;
- generation versus edit, variation, inpainting, outpainting, compositing, cleanup,
  or conversion;
- capability/provider discovery and honest unavailable-capability handling;
- reference-image roles and edit targets;
- translation of approved direction into a provider-ready prompt;
- explicit frame invariants and edit locks;
- controlled variant strategy;
- full-frame and detail inspection against the brief;
- targeted iteration that changes one defect without silently redesigning the image;
- output location/result, prompt trace, capability used, defects, provenance,
  licensing, and asset-custody handoff.

It should not decide a material crop, viewpoint, focus strategy, lighting concept,
palette story, or attention path when those remain creatively unresolved.

## What stays downstream

The visual-storytelling skills should not absorb:

- responsive CSS, component architecture, design tokens, or browser mechanics;
- WCAG conformance methods, semantic order, input behaviour, or reduced-motion
  implementation;
- Phaser/rendering implementation, tile-map algorithms, shader code, or game-system
  balancing;
- asset optimisation, hosting, final licensing decisions, or publication;
- canonical project facts not present in source evidence;
- imitation instructions for a living artist, studio, protected frame, or branded
  visual identity.

Those belong to existing implementation, accessibility, game, and asset-custody
skills. The creative skill supplies intent and invariants they must preserve.

## Evaluation obligations

A strong output should answer:

1. What changes for the audience?
2. At which composition scales does the story operate?
3. Who controls view, order, pace, and state?
4. What is noticed first, second, and last?
5. What is shown, withheld, or left off-frame?
6. Why this crop, viewpoint, perspective, and depth structure?
7. What do foreground, middle-ground, and background each contribute?
8. Is focus assigned deliberately or defaulted?
9. What does the light reveal, model, separate, or conceal?
10. How do colour roles support hierarchy and changed state?
11. What is still, what moves, and why?
12. How does this frame contribute to its page, sequence, route, or world?
13. Which constraints create useful invention rather than decorative imitation?
14. Which facts are evidenced, and which implications remain deliberately open?
15. What must downstream implementation preserve across sizes, states, and inputs?

## Research conclusion

The proposed skill system is justified, but its centre of gravity must remain
creative direction rather than medium taxonomy. `directing-visual-stories` should
route from story function to a small number of compositional families, then require
an authored frame/page/world contract. `generating-images` should execute that
contract faithfully and transparently as an acknowledged derivative of the Apache
2.0 OpenAI skill.

The system succeeds when an agent can explain why the crop is close, why the hand
matters, why the background remains visible, why one plane is sharp and another is
not, why an inset interrupts a larger field, why light changes at a threshold, why
motion is withheld until one decisive beat, why a landmark reads from several
routes, or why a palette cycles—then preserve that reasoning through generation or
implementation.
