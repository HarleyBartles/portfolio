# Assets

Assets are the images, icons, fonts, and social images that a portfolio carries. *Web Design in 4 Minutes* treats a header background and a logo as tools to establish identity. *The Shape of Design* treats design as a gift: the work becomes an experience when it is given to an audience, and attention is the scarcest resource. A generic or unoptimised asset wastes that attention and tells the visitor the work was assembled.

## Core lessons from the source

- Images are not ornaments unless they are used as ornaments. An image can support the content or be an active part of the message. If it does neither, it is weight.
- Choose assets for the format. Background images need a fallback colour, proper sizing, and a sensible position. `background-size: cover` is a good default for hero backgrounds when the image is large enough.
- Logos and identity. A logo should be treated with care: consistent sizing, alignment, and padding. It should not feel pasted in.
- Favicons and social previews are assets. A missing favicon or Open Graph image is a missing detail. These assets are part of the finish.
- Self-host or attribute. Fonts and icons should be self-hosted when possible, or used under a clear license. Do not rely on a CDN or service that could disappear or break the page.
- Optimise. Assets should be the right format and size for the job. A portfolio with uncompressed images looks careless even if the layout is good.
- Attention is the gift. *The Shape of Design* says attention is the scarcest resource and the most valuable gift. Every asset should earn the attention it demands.

## $10k vs $500

| $500 | $10k |
|---|---|
| Generic or unoptimised images | Original or intentionally chosen images, appropriate format and size |
| Favicon and social preview missing | All meta assets and favicons in place |
| Mixed icon styles | A single, consistent icon set |
| Assets loaded from unreliable third parties | Self-hosted or attribution-respecting fonts, icons, and images |
