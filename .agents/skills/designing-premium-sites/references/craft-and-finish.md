# Craft and finish

Craft is the evidence that a person cared. *The Shape of Design* links craft to beauty, saying the highest craft comes from affection for the audience. *Web Design in 4 Minutes* shows that small details - a background on a code block, the space around an inline `code` element, the transition on a hover - are what make a page feel finished. Craft and finish are the accumulation of these small decisions.

## Core lessons from the source

- Details signal care. Spacing, alignment, type rendering, hover states, loading behaviour, focus rings, and transitions all say whether the work was assembled or crafted. Treat these as features, not polish.
- Beauty is a special form of craft. The Shaker proverb says: if it is both necessary and useful, make it beautiful. Beauty is not an extra; it is the extra attention that shows the work was made with love.
- The "long, hard, stupid way." Great work goes beyond the minimum because the maker is proud of the craft. This extra essence is what makes the work worth experiencing.
- Attention is the scarcest resource. A crafted page respects the visitor's attention by removing friction and making every interaction predictable and pleasant.
- Finish is a layer. Just as *Resilient Web Design* treats design in layers, finish is the top layer that shows the lower layers were built correctly. Misalignment, missing focus states, and layout shifts betray the lower layers.

## $10k vs $500

| $500 | $10k |
|---|---|
| Misaligned, inconsistent spacing | Spacing and alignment snap to a scale and a grid |
| Hover and focus states are default or missing | Hover, focus, and loading states are considered and consistent |
| Layout shifts as assets load | No cumulative layout shift; the page feels stable |
| Details feel patched in | Details form a consistent, intentional surface |
