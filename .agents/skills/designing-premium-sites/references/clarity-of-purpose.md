# Clarity of purpose

Clarity of purpose is the answer to the Why behind every design choice. *The Shape of Design* shows that How questions improve craft, but Why questions reveal purpose. A portfolio that cannot explain why each section exists cannot argue for the author. Purpose is what turns a collection of projects into a coherent case for the engineer.

## Core lessons from the source

- Start with Why. Before asking how to style a section or build a component, ask why it needs to exist. If the answer is unclear, the section is clutter.
- Curate, don't catalogue. A premium portfolio is not a showcase of every technology the author has ever touched. It is a curated argument for the author's judgment and future fit.
- One job per section. Every section should answer "what do I want the reader to think or do next?" If a section does not move the reader toward that goal, it should be cut or merged.
- Message, tone, and format. *The Shape of Design* says good work has three traits: what is being said, how it is said, and the artifact. For each portfolio piece, these three must reinforce one another.
- Record the rationale. Why decisions are invisible in the finished piece, so they must be written down. Recording rationale keeps later agents from undoing the purpose for the sake of a trend or convenience.

## $10k vs $500

| $500 | $10k |
|---|---|
| Shows every project and technology | Curates a clear argument for the author's judgment |
| Sections with no clear job | Every section has a single intended outcome |
| "About me" instead of "why hire me" | Each paragraph moves the reader toward a goal |
| Decisions are unrecorded | Why is written down so later agents preserve it |
