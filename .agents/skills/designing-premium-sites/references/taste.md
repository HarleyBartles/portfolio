# Taste

Taste is the coherence that makes a portfolio feel authored. *The Shape of Design* says a good piece of design moves from maker to audience to others; it gains value as it passes between contexts. A portfolio with taste is one where the audience can sense the maker's judgment. It is not about expensive effects; it is about the right decisions made for the right reasons.

## Core lessons from the source

- Taste is coherence. The palette, type, layout, motion, and assets should feel like they belong to the same voice. A portfolio that copies unrelated trends will feel like a template.
- Taste is restraint. Say no to colours, fonts, and effects that do not fit the core message. A small, consistent vocabulary is better than a large, noisy one.
- Taste is purpose. Every choice should connect to the Why. If a choice cannot be explained, it probably does not belong.
- Taste is audience. *The Shape of Design* argues that design is made for other people. A portfolio with taste respects the audience: their attention, their device, their preferences, their time.
- Taste is a gift. The best design is a gift that the audience wants to pass on. It is made with attention, given with care, and received with recognition.
- The particular is the universal. Specific, true details about the author's work and process will resonate more than generic claims. A portfolio with taste tells a story only the author can tell.
