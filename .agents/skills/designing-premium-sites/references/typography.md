# Typography

Typography is the foundation of almost everything on the web. *Web Design in 4 Minutes* notes that written content makes up more than 90% of the web, so styling text is the highest-impact design work. *Resilient Web Design* adds that text is the most resilient material: it can be rendered on any device and in any context. Good typography in a portfolio is not decoration; it is the primary means of making the content legible, credible, and confident.

## Core lessons from the source

- Content first. The purpose of design is to enhance the presentation of content. Content should never be an afterthought. A portfolio's text must be readable before it is beautiful.
- Measure. Long lines of text are hard to read. A sensible `max-width` (around `50em` in *Web Design in 4 Minutes*) keeps the eye from getting lost.
- Line height and spacing. Generous line height (around `1.5`) and comfortable padding around the body make the page feel open. Heading spacing should be handled with a scale, not arbitrary margins.
- Choose fonts deliberately. The default serif or a font stack without a reason signals that no choice was made. A modern sans-serif like Helvetica or Arial, or a robust serif like Georgia, gives the page immediate polish. A custom font can add identity, but it should load well and have good fallbacks.
- Contrast in weight. *Web Design in 4 Minutes* uses a darker shade (`#333`) for headings and strong text, while the body is softer (`#555`). This keeps hierarchy clear without harsh contrast.
- Avoid fashion that hurts. All-caps, very thin weights, and tiny text can look stylish but damage readability. The type scale should serve the content, not the trend.

## $10k vs $500

| $500 | $10k |
|---|---|
| Default fonts, default sizes | A considered pair of fonts with clear roles |
| All-caps labels, thin headings that are hard to read | Scale and weight guide the eye; body text is comfortable at all widths |
| Text dumps with no hierarchy | Chunked content, clear headings, and short paragraphs |
| Body text is pure black on white | Softer body text with darker headings for emphasis |
