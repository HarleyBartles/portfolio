# Authority record for writing-portfolio-articles

This skill is a first-party Portfolio editorial method informed by the sources below. The sources are citation-only evidence: none is vendored or redistributed here, and none outranks the Portfolio writing policy or Harley's editorial judgement.

## Editorial craft and reader experience

### `PURDUE-PARAGRAPHS` — Purdue OWL, “On Paragraphs”

- URL: https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/academic_writing/paragraphs_and_paragraphing/index.html
- Retrieved: 2026-09-22
- Helps answer: when a paragraph is unified, developed and ready to yield to a new idea or reader pause.
- Informs: paragraph-boundary diagnosis, coherence, development and the warning that short paragraphs deserve inspection.
- Limit: introductory academic-writing guidance, not a rule that every editorial paragraph needs a topic sentence or minimum length.
- Follow further when: a paragraph pass cannot distinguish intentional pace from underdevelopment.

### `PURDUE-SENTENCES` — Purdue Writing Lab, “Sentence Structure, Variety, and Clarity”

- URL: https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/graduate_writing/introduction_to_writing/documents/revising-and-editing/sentence-structure-activity.pdf
- Retrieved: 2026-09-22
- Helps answer: how clause relationships, sentence variety and deliberate contrast change weight, clarity and emphasis.
- Informs: the full-stop diagnostic, purposeful conjunction, cadence variation and the preservation of an earned short sentence.
- Limit: an academic exercise and grammatical primer, not a house rhythm or a command to maximise structural variety.
- Follow further when: a line edit changes logical weight or produces either choppy declarations or over-joined prose.

### `POYNTER-TOOLS` — Roy Peter Clark, “Thirty Tools for Writers”

- URL: https://www.poynter.org/archive/2002/thirty-tools-for-writers/
- Retrieval checked: 2026-09-22; the page rejected the automated client with HTTP 403.
- Helps answer: which practical techniques can sharpen emphasis, order, detail, pace and endings.
- Informs: reading for movement, concrete detail, sentence emphasis and endings that stop with purpose.
- Limit: a practitioner toolkit, not a mandatory checklist; its individual tools must remain subordinate to form and voice.
- Follow further when: the article is technically sound but lacks movement, memorable detail or a decisive finish.

### `POYNTER-LEADS` — Roy Peter Clark, “Lead vs. lede”

- URL: https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2019/lead-vs-lede-roy-peter-clark-has-the-definitive-answer-at-last/
- Retrieval checked: 2026-09-22; the page rejected the automated client with HTTP 403.
- Helps answer: how journalistic opening terminology and practice developed.
- Informs: the distinction between opening technique and empty urgency, without imposing newsroom vocabulary on Portfolio articles.
- Limit: a historical and practitioner discussion, not a complete theory of openings.
- Follow further when: an opening uses a journalistic lead, anecdotal entry or delayed orientation whose conventions matter.

### `NIEMAN-THEME` — Jack Hart, “Nut grafs: Getting to the heart of the story”

- URL: https://niemanstoryboard.org/2021/10/19/nut-grafs-getting-to-the-heart-of-the-story/
- Retrieved: 2026-09-22
- Helps answer: how a private theme or focus statement can locate a story's centre and guide reporting and structure.
- Informs: the theme sentence, reader promise, centre-of-gravity diagnosis and the distinction between an editorial tool and compulsory published copy.
- Limit: written from narrative journalism; not every essay needs a visible nut graf.
- Follow further when: strong material accumulates without one legible meaning or governing question.

### `NIEMAN-FORMS` — “News Feature v. Narrative: What's the Difference?”

- URL: https://niemanstoryboard.org/2006/01/09/news-feature-v-narrative-whats-the-difference/
- Retrieved: 2026-09-22
- Helps answer: how narrative and feature forms create different reader expectations and organise material differently.
- Informs: choosing form from material, naming a governing form and controlling hybrid transitions.
- Limit: compares two journalistic forms; the Portfolio taxonomy is a broader first-party synthesis.
- Follow further when: a reported, narrative or hybrid article keeps changing its contract with the reader.

### `NIEMAN-STRUCTURE` — “Sticking a story together — and nailing the structure”

- URL: https://niemanstoryboard.org/2022/02/02/sticking-a-story-together-and-nailing-the-structure/
- Retrieved: 2026-09-22
- Helps answer: how scenes, sections and transitions can be sequenced around a governing structural logic.
- Informs: section jobs, movement maps, transitions and the requirement that structure change the reader's understanding.
- Limit: craft reporting and examples require adaptation to technical essays, tutorials and cumulative lists.
- Follow further when: every section is individually sound but the article does not accumulate or turn.

### `NNG-WEB-READING` — Nielsen Norman Group, “How Users Read on the Web”

- URL: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/
- Retrieved: 2026-09-22
- Helps answer: how scanning, concise presentation and information-bearing headings affect web reading.
- Informs: headings-only review, paragraph openings, meaningful links and inspection of the article as a rendered web object.
- Limit: usability findings do not require every reader to scan or justify flattening long-form editorial prose into fragments.
- Follow further when: continuous prose works on the page but route hierarchy, headings or link presentation fail a scanning reader.

### `GOOGLE-SELF-EDITING` — Google Technical Writing, “Self-editing”

- URL: https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/two/editing
- Retrieved: 2026-09-22
- Helps answer: how staged self-editing can separate organisation, clarity and sentence-level repair.
- Informs: the outside-in pass order, concise revision questions and final recovery check.
- Limit: technical-writing instruction optimises for explanatory clarity, not the full range of narrative voice, ambiguity or dramatic effect.
- Follow further when: an explanatory or tutorial article needs a disciplined clarity pass after its argument is stable.

## Language, AI fatigue and classification

### `EMNLP-HUMAN-LLM` — Muñoz-Ortiz et al., “Linguistic and Embedding-Based Profiling of Texts Generated by Humans and Large Language Models”

- URL: https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1163/
- Retrieved: 2026-09-22
- Helps answer: which aggregate linguistic differences have been observed between sampled human and LLM-generated text.
- Informs: the bounded watch for homogenisation and repeated model-favourite structures.
- Limit: population-level profiling cannot identify authorship, prove that a passage is bad, or justify detector scores and token bans.
- Follow further when: a proposed fatigue rule risks turning a research observation into a universal prose verdict.

### `PMC-AI-WRITING` — peer-reviewed research archived as PMC11874169

- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11874169/
- Retrieval checked: 2026-09-22; the automated client encountered a browser challenge.
- Helps answer: how widespread generative-AI assistance may alter recurring vocabulary and writing practice at corpus scale.
- Informs: the distinction between contextual fatigue signals and proof of authorship or quality.
- Limit: corpus-level association does not establish the origin, intent or editorial value of an individual sentence.
- Follow further when: a recurrence finding is being treated as causal evidence or an accusation of AI authorship.

### `BBFC-12A` — British Board of Film Classification, “12A/12 — Suitable for 12 years and over”

- URL: https://www.bbfc.co.uk/rating/12
- Retrieved: 2026-09-22
- Helps answer: how context, frequency and whole-work effect govern 12A treatment of language and other content categories.
- Informs: the Portfolio's explicitly 12A-inspired whole-site calibration and its contextual rather than purely numerical review.
- Limit: the Portfolio has not been classified by the BBFC; the repository's stricter language limits are first-party policy.
- Follow further when: public copy introduces strong language or relevant dangerous behaviour, discrimination, drugs, nudity, sex, sexual violence, self-harm, threat, horror or violence.

## Derivation boundary

- First-party synthesis: the Portfolio form taxonomy, editorial brief, pass order, section-job method, sentence and paragraph diagnostics, authored-voice rules, fatigue classifications, recovery check, field-learning lifecycle and research-routing decisions.
- Repository policy: the voice contract, prohibited punctuation, 12A-inspired site limits, authority order and human decision boundaries belong to `.agents/doctrine/writing-policy.md`.
- Citation-only evidence: the linked works support or challenge parts of the method but are not copied into this repository.
- Outside scope: authorship detection, universal quality scoring, mandatory templates, mechanical style enforcement, imitation of the published corpus, and claims that applying the skill necessarily improves prose.

## Reconciliation

- Where introductory clarity guidance conflicts with an intentional editorial effect, preserve the effect when its reader benefit is defensible.
- Where web-scanning advice conflicts with continuous long-form reading, test both modes instead of declaring one universal reader behaviour.
- Where corpus research identifies aggregate tendencies, use them as contextual watch classes, never individual verdicts.
- Where an external source conflicts with Portfolio doctrine or explicit human intent, surface the conflict; do not silently replace repository authority.
