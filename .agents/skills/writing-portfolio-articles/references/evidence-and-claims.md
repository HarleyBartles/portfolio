# Evidence and Claims

Classify consequential claims at the smallest useful scope:

- **Demonstrated fact:** inspectable in the work or evidence.
- **Sourced fact:** supported by a named external source.
- **Recollection:** honestly remembered, not independently established.
- **Inference:** a conclusion drawn from known observations.
- **Interpretation:** the author's reading of events or artefacts.
- **Opinion:** an explicit judgement or preference.

Use technical specificity only when it is accurate. Qualify the uncertain clause, not the entire paragraph. Do not turn every sentence into a disclaimer, and do not use confidence of tone as evidence.

For each externally checkable claim, record what supports it and place the link where it helps a sceptical reader inspect the claim. A link is not ornamental authority.

Watch for:

- causal claims supported only by chronology;
- remembered dialogue silently presented as verbatim;
- metrics without date, scope or denominator;
- implementation detail generalised into universal advice;
- public claims that expose private identities, coordinates or systems;
- a stronger claim introduced during a style edit.

If evidence is missing, mark the gap or narrow the claim. Never write smoothly around it.
