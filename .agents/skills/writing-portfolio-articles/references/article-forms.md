# Article Forms

Choose form after identifying the material and reader, not because the last article used it.

| Form | Best suited to | Main failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Argument | A contestable proposition | Assertions arranged as certainty |
| Technical case study | A decision under real constraints | Chronology without meaning |
| Reflective narrative | Experience whose interpretation changes | Memoir without a reader payoff |
| Reported feature | Several sources or viewpoints | Research presented without a governing question |
| Field note | A bounded observation worth sharing now | Pretending provisional thought is settled |
| Tutorial or explanation | A reader needs usable understanding | Steps without conceptual bearings |
| Making-of story | Choices embodied in a designed result | Process diary or promotional reveal |
| Comparison | A decision needs explicit criteria | Feature matrix without judgement |
| Interview or Q&A | Another person's thinking is the material | Questions that do not accumulate |
| Cumulative list | Separate entries build one larger argument | Interchangeable answers with no movement |

Hybrids are legitimate. Name the governing form and the job of each secondary form. Signal transitions when reader expectations change.

Ask:

- What can this material prove or reveal?
- What experience should the reader have while learning it?
- What would this form tempt us to fake, repeat or omit?
- Does the ending belong to the chosen form?
