# Editorial Brief

Before drafting, write a private brief short enough to remain useful:

- **Reader:** who arrives, and what can they already understand?
- **Occasion:** why this piece now?
- **Theme sentence:** what is the article really about?
- **Change:** what should be different for the reader by the end?
- **Promise:** what does the opening commit the article to deliver?
- **Material:** incidents, mechanisms, images, sources and constraints available.
- **Evidence gap:** what still needs proof or careful qualification?
- **Form:** why this form fits better than its nearest alternative.
- **Ending intent:** resolve, return, widen, sharpen or leave a bounded afterimage?
- **Non-scope:** what adjacent article are we deliberately not writing?

The theme sentence is a tool, not compulsory published copy. Test every substantial section against it: serve, complicate, counterpoint or remove.

Do not use the brief to pre-author every conclusion. Its job is to keep the draft honest while leaving room for discovery.
