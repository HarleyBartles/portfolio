# Structure and Movement

A section earns its existence by changing the reader's understanding. Give each section a verb: establish, demonstrate, complicate, test, reveal, narrow, widen, decide or close.

Map the draft as a sequence of section jobs. Then test:

- Is the governing logic chronological, causal, argumentative, thematic or deliberately braided?
- Does each section create a reason to read the next?
- Are scene, mechanism, evidence, explanation and abstraction in useful proportion?
- Does the piece change scale deliberately rather than lurching?
- Can a headings-only read recover the argument?
- Does any section restate the conclusion already earned by the one before it?

Transitions may be carried by chronology, consequence, a repeated image, an unanswered question or a change in scale. Do not add connective phrases merely to announce that prose continues.

For cumulative lists, each answer must add pressure, range or consequence to the whole. Reorder entries to reveal an argument; if any answer can move anywhere without cost, the list probably lacks movement.
