# Site, threshold, procession, and embodied viewing

Use this route when the audience's body, route, and position are part of the
composition. Architecture, landscape, and installation treat arrival, enclosure,
material transition, light, and movement through a site as authored story.

## Compose the procession

Map the encounter as bodily states rather than screenshots:

1. **Approach:** what is visible before entry, and what expectation forms?
2. **Threshold:** what changes in access, light, sound implication, material,
   ceiling, direction, or social permission?
3. **Compression:** where does the field narrow, darken, lower, obstruct, or slow?
4. **Release:** where does it widen, brighten, rise, reveal, or permit orientation?
5. **Dwell:** what invites pause, gathering, inspection, rest, or choice?
6. **Return/departure:** what looks or means differently after the encounter?

A threshold has thickness. It may stage hesitation, concealment, preparation, or
change of role. Do not reduce it to a line between sections or rooms.

## Author the body-site relation

Record eye level, reach, walking speed, turning radius, likely posture, and whether
the audience feels exposed, sheltered, elevated, compressed, invited, or excluded.
Compose in section as well as plan: ceiling height, vertical opening, overlook,
stair, drop, canopy, and horizon can dominate the experience.

Site specificity means the work depends on an existing condition: architecture,
weather, history, circulation, view, social use, or neighbouring object. Name that
condition and the consequence of moving the work elsewhere. For responsive pages,
the analogous site includes viewport, scroll position, surrounding content, input
mode, and return path.

Light and material change with time and movement. Decide whether the audience sees
the source, follows reflected light, crosses from warm to cool, or discovers wear
where other bodies have passed. Landscape may use framed or borrowed views, seasonal
change, terrain, vegetation, and distance as part of the authored field.

## Embodied viewing contract

Add an encounter mode to `directed`, `guided`, or `exploratory` viewing:

- **pictorial:** primarily read as a bounded surface;
- **spatial:** meaning accumulates across viewpoints;
- **embodied:** posture, movement, scale, or physical relation changes the read;
- **participatory:** audience action changes the field or becomes visible within it.

### Route output

Record: site condition; encounter mode; approach, threshold, compression, release,
dwell, and departure; plan and section relationships; body-scale cues; material and
light transitions; rest/orientation points; alternate route; and the experiential
invariant that must survive implementation.

### Avoid

- designing one perfect view for a route-based encounter;
- treating thresholds as decorative dividers;
- using architecture language without body scale, material, or movement;
- forcing every route through the same reveal when agency is promised;
- claiming an accessible encounter from visual intent alone; pass operational
  access, navigation, and testing to the responsible implementation skills.
