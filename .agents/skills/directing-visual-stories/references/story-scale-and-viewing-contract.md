# Story scale and viewing contract

Composition is nested. Decide which scale carries the beat, then check the
containing field or encounter before treating a local decision as successful:

`mark/material -> object/body -> frame/surface -> field/site -> encounter/sequence`

- **Mark/material** is a line, stain, seam, texture, fold, edge, joint, or patch of
  light. It carries process, force, atmosphere, or evidence.
- **Object/body** is a prop, figure, garment, sculpture, tile, word, or landmark.
  It carries identity, action, material behaviour, or motif.
- **Frame/surface** is a bounded image, shot, panel, inset, canvas, map, viewport,
  or screen region. It controls a moment, point of view, or worked plane.
- **Field/site** is the whole image, page, spread, stage picture, screen, room,
  exhibition, town, or navigable scene. It controls relations among local parts and
  the audience's position.
- **Encounter/sequence** is change across panels, cuts, scroll, handling, cues,
  animation, movement, route, state, or revisitation. It controls anticipation,
  memory, before/after, and participation.

Name the smallest scale at which the audience can understand the intended change,
and the larger scale that gives it context. A hand may be the frame's subject,
but the full image must still explain whose hand it is, what it touches, and what
the crop makes us infer. A panel may be legible alone, but its page position can
change its timing, emphasis, or relation to the next panel.

## Viewing contracts

The audience may control different parts of the read:

| Contract | Author controls | Composition must provide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `directed` | view, order, and often timing | precise reveal, crop, focus transfer, and beat |
| `guided` | main route and hierarchy; audience controls pace | landmarks, thresholds, rhythm, and hierarchy that survive dwelling |
| `exploratory` | rules and affordances; audience chooses view/order | readable landmarks, multiple viable observation points, state evidence, and orientation |

Do not import directed-film precision into an exploratory world without a recovery
plan. Do not flatten a directed image into a collection of equally available
details. The contract is a creative constraint: it determines where ambiguity is
useful and where the work must explain itself.

When position and movement matter, add an encounter mode:

| Mode | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `pictorial` | primarily understood as one bounded surface or privileged view |
| `spatial` | meaning accumulates across several viewpoints |
| `embodied` | bodily scale, posture, movement, or proximity changes the read |
| `participatory` | audience action changes the field or makes the audience part of it |

Viewing control and encounter mode are separate. A guided handscroll is pictorial
and handled over time; an exploratory game world is spatial; an installation may be
guided and embodied; an interactive exhibition may be exploratory and participatory.

## Route questions

Before choosing a composition family, answer:

1. What should change in the audience: knowledge, feeling, anticipation,
   orientation, relationship, or agency?
2. What is the beat at mark/material, object/body, frame/surface, field/site, and
   encounter/sequence scales?
3. What is shown, withheld, delayed, or left off-frame?
4. Who owns the view, and can the audience look elsewhere or return later?
5. What must survive a crop, viewport change, state change, or player route?

If the answer is only “make it cinematic/comic/editorial,” keep the direction
open and route by story function. Medium labels are not composition decisions.

## Output implication

Record `scale`, `viewing contract`, `encounter mode` when relevant, `audience
change`, `beat`, `containing field/site`, and `survival invariant`. Then route to
only the references that solve the actual problem.
