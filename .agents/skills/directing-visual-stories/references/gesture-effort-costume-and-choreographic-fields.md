# Gesture, effort, costume, and choreographic fields

Use this route when motion quality, held stillness, ensemble relation, silhouette,
or clothing carries the story. Theatre blocking says where bodies are; choreography
and costume explain how they move, resist, pause, gather, and transform.

## Give movement a quality

Describe motion through observable force and timing:

- heavy or light;
- sustained or sudden;
- direct or indirect;
- bound or free;
- accelerating, suspended, collapsing, resisting, rebounding, or trembling.

Pair the quality with a story verb. A direct, heavy crossing can confront; a bound,
indirect retreat can conceal; a suspended reach can defer a decision. Avoid generic
“dynamic motion” that supplies energy without meaning.

Stillness has duration. State what continues around the held body, what tension is
stored, and what releases it. A pose is not merely a keyframe when the audience must
feel how long it is held or what effort maintains it.

## Compose the choreographic field

Use pathways, facing, spacing, rhythm, and repetition to build relation:

- **unison:** shared purpose, conformity, ritual, or force;
- **canon:** transmission, pursuit, consequence, or delayed agreement;
- **counterpoint:** simultaneous difference whose relation remains legible;
- **accumulation:** repeated gesture gains bodies, weight, or meaning;
- **interruption:** one body or event breaks the established score;
- **counterflow:** a subject moves against the field and becomes evidence.

For guided or exploratory work, include the audience in the score. A body, object,
animation, or obstruction may slow, divert, invite, or expose the viewer.

## Costume as moving form

Direct costume through silhouette, construction, material, and permitted movement.
Stiff, padded, trailing, transparent, weighted, repaired, oversized, or tightly
bound garments change both identity and action. Drape can reveal or conceal anatomy;
folds can record gravity and motion; wear can locate labour and history.

Check macro and micro scales: the silhouette must communicate at distance, while
fasteners, seams, patches, tools, and material response reward proximity. State what
must remain recognisable when the figure is back-facing, partly occluded, moving,
or reduced to a thumbnail.

### Route output

Record: action and movement quality; effort and tempo; pathway; stillness duration;
ensemble device; audience relation; silhouette; garment structure, drape, and wear;
identity details by viewing distance; and the gesture/silhouette invariant.

### Avoid

- confusing pose with movement quality;
- choreographing spectacle without changed relation or knowledge;
- designing clothing as surface decoration detached from body and action;
- adding fabric motion that contradicts force, gravity, or direction;
- relying on facial expression when silhouette, posture, or costume must carry the
  beat at distance.
