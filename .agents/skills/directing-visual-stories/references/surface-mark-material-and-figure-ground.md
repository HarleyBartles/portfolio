# Surface, mark, material, and figure-ground

Use this route when the visual story is carried by the worked surface itself, not
only by subjects arranged inside an illusionistic window. Painting, printmaking,
collage, textile, calligraphy, and graphic abstraction make colour, edge, mark,
support, and material part of the evidence.

## Choose the surface contract

Decide whether the field should behave primarily as:

- **window:** depth and depicted space dominate;
- **plane:** shape, interval, pattern, and edge remain visibly flat;
- **material:** grain, weave, stain, cut, seam, relief, or construction carries
  meaning;
- **collision:** incompatible images, scales, times, or realities remain visibly
  joined rather than reconciled;
- **atmosphere:** colour and value envelop before a subject resolves.

These modes can coexist, but name the tension. A flat patterned figure against a
deep setting may assert memory, symbol, or otherness. A collage seam may tell the
audience that two realities have been forced together. A page-wide colour field may
establish dread or release before any copy is read.

## Compose marks and ground

Marks have direction, pressure, density, rhythm, and residue. Use them to imply
labour, speed, hesitation, erosion, repetition, repair, or interruption. Preserve
productive differences between a crisp edge, a bleeding edge, a cut edge, and an
erased edge. Do not request generic texture when the story depends on how the mark
was made or what it suggests happened.

Figure-ground may be stable, reversible, or unresolved. Stable separation supports
recognition and action. Reversible figure-ground can make absence, camouflage,
duality, or contested ownership active. An all-over field can refuse a single
centre and reward dwelling, but it still needs a discoverable relation or rhythm.

Material is not a style label. Cloth can carry domestic labour, portability,
repair, inheritance, softness, or enclosure. Gold, paper, brick dust, newsprint,
ink, and exposed support each bring histories and physical behaviour. Use only
meanings supported by the brief or source context; do not invent cultural symbolism.

## Scale, abstraction, and simultaneity

State the intended viewing distance. A monumental field can envelop; a hand-sized
surface can invite intimacy; a detail can turn material into landscape. Test the
macro composition and the micro surface separately.

Abstraction can carry story through force, rhythm, interval, balance, rupture,
temperature, or accumulation without depicting a literal event. Collage and flat
pattern can also hold interior/exterior, past/present, real/imagined, or public/
private simultaneously. Do not force a causal scene when affective or symbolic
coexistence is the intended story.

### Route output

Record: surface contract; figure-ground relation; dominant mark and edge behaviour;
material and supported meaning; colour/value field; scale and viewing distance;
depth-versus-flatness tension; repetition or rupture; and the surface invariant that
must survive generation, responsive translation, or animation.

### Avoid

- treating painting as photography with visible brush texture;
- adding grain, paper, cloth, or collage seams as decorative nostalgia;
- confusing ambiguity with an absence of hierarchy or relation;
- assigning cultural meaning to colour, pattern, or material without authority;
- letting image generation smooth away the seam, edge, ground, or material event
  that carries the story.
