# Curation, juxtaposition, and interpretive paths

Use this route when meaning emerges from selecting, withholding, grouping, and
placing several objects, images, texts, or experiences. Exhibition design and
curation treat adjacency, anchors, labels, pacing, and visitor choice as composition.

## Build the argument through selection

State the proposition and choose the smallest set of objects or frames that can
carry it. Each item should act as anchor, evidence, counterexample, bridge, context,
or release. An attractive item without a role is clutter; an omitted item may be an
important editorial decision.

Juxtaposition can:

- reveal similarity or difference;
- make chronology, causality, or inheritance visible;
- challenge a category;
- let one object lend scale, materiality, or emotional weight to another;
- expose contradiction between claim and evidence.

Record what the audience should infer from the adjacency. Do not assume proximity
will explain itself.

## Compose anchors and paths

Provide a few anchor objects or fields that carry the proposition for audiences who
skim or enter midway. Cluster subordinate material around a recoverable throughline.
Use repeated colour, form, line, label treatment, or spatial relation as a relay,
but do not make every cluster identical.

Design for more than one pace:

- **glance:** proposition and anchor are available immediately;
- **browse:** clusters and comparisons remain legible out of order;
- **study:** detail, provenance, qualification, and contradiction reward dwelling;
- **return:** earlier material changes meaning after later evidence.

Labels, captions, controls, and evidence must have clear ownership. Keep the link
between interpretive text and object visible without letting explanation overpower
the object. Provide breathing room, rest, and sensory relief where density would
otherwise flatten the story.

For a page, treat the scroll as an exhibition route rather than a compulsory linear
read. For a game hub, treat landmarks, characters, props, and interaction sites as
curated evidence. For an image brief, use a deliberate constellation of objects
rather than prop accumulation.

### Route output

Record: proposition; included and deliberately omitted material; anchor objects;
clusters and item roles; key juxtapositions and intended inference; throughline and
relay cue; glance/browse/study/return paths; label/evidence ownership; rest points;
and the curatorial invariant that must survive reflow or route choice.

### Avoid

- treating completeness as stronger than selection;
- placing objects together without naming the inferred relation;
- making labels or cards carry a hierarchy the objects do not support;
- assuming every visitor starts at the top or follows the same route;
- allowing density, novelty, or evidence volume to erase rest and orientation.
