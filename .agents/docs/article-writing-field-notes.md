## Purpose

Record the outcomes that may help the article-writing skill improve over time. Consult these notes when a current article presents a relevant problem, but verify every candidate against the article in front of you and the current writing doctrine.

Do not add an entry after every invocation. Record a note only for a meaningful correction, rejection, mixed outcome, missed issue, surprising success, useful abstention, new form, conflict or overcorrection.

## Promotion

```text
field use
→ human correction or observed success
→ candidate lesson
→ classify the likely owner
→ reproduce against current live work when useful
→ update the smallest authority
→ recheck affected work
```

One occurrence is normally recorded, not promoted. Recurrence across materially different work may justify a focused review. Harley may explicitly promote a single well-reasoned lesson after inspecting the article and its evidence. A clear conflict with existing authority requires repair to the smallest owning authority and a recheck of affected work. Doctrine changes only through Harley's explicit editorial decision. The skill does not rewrite itself.

Consolidate this log when it gets in the way of use. Do not invent a time, entry-count or file-size trigger.

## Entries

### Candidate: opening beat earns continuation

- **Article or PR:** `agentic-engineering-vs-vibe-coding`, PR #75
- **Editorial stage:** opening revision and accepted-draft retrospective
- **Observation:** The first replacement opening lost the original paragraph's distinctive point of view. Restoring that paragraph improved the frozen cohort's opening trajectories. Making agentic engineering visible in the opening improved them again. The panel located possible losses; reading the openings explained what each revision gave or withheld.
- **Outcome:** improved after recovery and a clearer title promise
- **Human correction:** Harley restored the original first paragraph, asked the opening to pay off both sides of the title, and accepted a durable opening-beat review and an accepted-article retrospective as ordinary workflow.
- **Scope:** system-wide diagnostic, without requiring a fixed opening form or literal title repetition
- **Candidate owner:** doctrine for the reader-attention principle, playbook for the review and retrospective steps, skill reference for diagnosis
- **Promotion evidence:** Harley's explicit editorial decision in the PR #75 drafting discussion. The lesson is grounded in the editorial reading; simulated readers remain advisory.

### Candidate: keep visual review tied to visual change

- **Article or PR:** `agentic-engineering-vs-vibe-coding`, PR #75
- **Editorial stage:** publication review
- **Observation:** A policy written for material visual or editorial changes caused a full viewport, motion and zoom checklist to be applied to an article rewrite in an unchanged reading shell. That review did not test the editorial claim; the focused rendered check did catch obsolete article-specific artwork.
- **Outcome:** changed but not improved by the broad checklist
- **Human correction:** Harley said copy edits do not require shell visual re-inspection. Removal of redundant article-specific art warrants only a focused check for an empty slot.
- **Scope:** repository workflow and presentation review boundary
- **Candidate owner:** design policy and article-writing playbook
- **Promotion evidence:** Harley's explicit correction during PR #75 review.
