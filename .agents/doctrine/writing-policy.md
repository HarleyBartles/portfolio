# Portfolio Writing Policy

Status: active policy
Owner: Portfolio repository
Scope: public editorial writing, including articles, case-study prose, summaries, standfirsts, titles, headings, captions, alt text and public route copy
Authority: binding repository policy for authored public prose; truth, safety, privacy, accessibility, legal boundaries and explicit human intent outrank style
Routed from: `/AGENTS.md`

Use this policy whenever public prose is drafted, revised, reviewed or published. Private engineering documents, commit messages and internal agent artifacts are outside the public-content rating scope.

## Authority and reader contract

Editorial decisions follow this order:

1. truth, safety, privacy, accessibility and legal boundaries;
2. explicit human intent;
3. accurate claims and honest provenance;
4. the reader promise and central meaning;
5. clarity and coherence;
6. authorised voice;
7. editorial finish; and
8. fatigue heuristics.

Every article must reward the time it asks for. It establishes a relationship with the reader, makes a promise it can keep, trusts the reader to follow necessary complexity, and does not waste attention on throat-clearing or repeated conclusions. Clarity need not flatten personality, and accessibility need not produce generic prose. No style repair may strengthen a claim, erase uncertainty, invent a memory or override a deliberate authorial choice without a concrete reader benefit.

## Centre of gravity and form

Before drafting or structural revision, state privately what the piece is really about and what changes for the reader by the end. This theme sentence is an editorial tool, not required published copy. Material that does not serve, complicate or deliberately counterpoint that centre needs a reason to remain.

Form follows the material. Available working forms include argument, technical case study, reflective narrative, reported feature, field note, tutorial or explanation, design or making-of story, comparison or evaluation, interview or Q&A, and a list with a cumulative argument. They are not mandatory templates. Hybrid forms are legitimate when their transitions and reader expectations are deliberately controlled.

## Evidence and epistemic honesty

Preserve the distinction between demonstrated fact, sourced fact, recollection, inference, interpretation and opinion. Technical specificity matters only when it is accurate. State uncertainty at the smallest useful scope: do not bluff, but do not bury the prose in disclaimers. A link should support the nearby claim rather than decorate it with borrowed authority.

## Authored voice

Voice comes from perspective, selection, specificity, judgement and rhythm. Slang, swearing, fragments and jokes may contribute, but none substitutes for something particular to say. The writing may be candid, funny, technical, self-implicating and opinionated. It must not manufacture intimacy, certainty, rebellion or cleverness.

The authorised voice includes natural contractions, directness, technical specificity, honest rough edges and dry humour. Prefer the shortest natural version of a thought, make contrasts concrete and allow a short sentence to finish a point when it is done. Public prose does not use em dashes. These are explicit preferences, not material from which to reverse-engineer a larger imitation profile.

## Openings, structure and movement

An opening establishes focus, authority, direction, voice and the writer-reader relationship. It may begin with action, a question, an image, a claim, a contradiction, a scene, a problem or a decision, provided it orients the reader before confusion becomes work. It must not manufacture urgency the article cannot repay.

Sections change the reader's understanding rather than divide word count. Each section has a job: establishing context, advancing or complicating the argument, providing evidence, testing an objection, changing scale, moving through time, turning towards a consequence, or preparing and delivering the ending. Sequence may be chronological, causal, argumentative, thematic or deliberately braided, but its governing logic should be legible.

Balance scene, evidence, explanation and abstraction according to the chosen form. Headings should survive a headings-only read. Transitions may come from chronology, consequence, a repeated image, an unresolved question or a change in scale; they do not always need an explicit connective phrase.

## Paragraphs

A paragraph is a unit of thought, movement or dramatic emphasis. During revision, ask each paragraph why it is not part of the paragraph before or after it.

One-sentence paragraphs are suspicious, not forbidden. Isolation must earn its place through emphasis, pace, transition, revelation or another specific editorial effect. Repeated short paragraphs can become mannerism just as dense blocks can become obstruction. Useful boundaries include changes of subject, time, scene, speaker, claim, evidence, scale, rhetorical function or pace. Paragraph openings should orient a scanning reader without making continuous reading schematic.

## Sentences and rhythm

During revision, ask each full stop why the material is not better joined by a comma, semicolon, colon, conjunction or another adjunctive construction. This is a diagnostic question, not a command to join.

A sentence boundary earns its place through meaning, pace, emphasis, clarity or voice. Short sentences and fragments may create impact, relief, wit or finality. Long and compound sentences may carry relationship, qualification and accumulating thought. Purposeful variation matters more than any preferred length. Reject both reflexively splitting related thoughts into choppy declarations and joining sentences until grammatical connection erases natural editorial cadence.

## Endings

An ending should feel caused by the article. It may resolve, return, widen, sharpen or leave a deliberate afterimage. It may answer the opening question, return to an image with changed meaning, deliver a consequence, leave a bounded unresolved question, offer a decision or next move, or stop on the strongest remaining concrete detail.

Avoid generic uplift, recap by default and conclusions that restate the standfirst. Cover the final paragraph and ask whether the article has already ended; if it has, remove the surplus ending.

## Corpus distinctiveness

Published work is evidence and a watch set, not a golden voice dataset. The corpus is strongest when it uses specific engineering incidents, implicates the author in mistakes, names mechanisms and consequences, distinguishes memory from demonstrated fact, finds dry humour in the material, closes its opening argument and gives sceptical readers inspectable links.

Watch for repeated antitheses; vocabulary around earning, rent, boundaries, machinery, shape, trust, evidence, the next engineer and "the work"; aphoristic block quotes; claim-anecdote-maxim-qualification sequences; headings that underserve scanners; title-echo endings; repeated incident families; lessons explained after the story has proved them; pressure for every section to contain a quotable line; and clusters of fragments or questions. Recurrence is a prompt for judgement, not proof of a defect. A repeated story earns its return only by revealing a materially different facet.

## AI-fatigue policy

AI fatigue is a constant watch class. Review for homogenised cadence, repeated rhetorical scaffolds, thoughtless symmetrical lists, abstraction where a mechanism belongs, generic scene-setting or uplift, excessive signposting, unearned certainty, false intimacy, conspicuous polish without individual judgement, and imitation of surface quirks from earlier work.

Also watch for synthetic profundity, empty antithesis, generic emotional emphasis, repeated rhetorical triplets, uniform section architecture, explanatory tails after the point has landed, conclusions inflated beyond the evidence, stock reversal frames and random variation added merely to "sound human". Familiar model-favourite phrases are warnings rather than a mechanical blacklist; assess their function and frequency across the public corpus.

Do not add decorative emoji to headings, status labels, lists or calls to action. An emoji belongs only when it is critical to the medium, quoted material or meaning under discussion. Treat scaffolds such as `I keep returning to`, `at its core`, `what matters is`, `the real story`, `this is where`, `the bottom line` and `it is worth noting` as warnings, including close paraphrases with the same rhetorical function. One earned use may be natural; recurrence across unrelated pieces can falsely present a model preference as Harley's vocabulary. Do not add a qualifying tail after a sentence has landed, apologise for honest project state or wrap evidence in defensive credibility claims.

The remedy is renewed authorship: recover the proposition, evidence, decisions and consequences. Thesaurus substitution, arbitrary roughening and detector scores do not establish authorship.

Read continuous prose aloud. Search the whole public corpus for banned punctuation, decorative emoji, stock phrases, repeated sentence skeletons and close semantic variants. When a sentence feels polished but impersonal, test whether removing its final qualification or replacing abstraction with the concrete actor or decision restores the point. Preserve supplied language and factual boundaries. Leave a genuinely load-bearing voice uncertainty for Harley rather than smoothing it into generic prose.

## Anti-overcorrection and recovery

No local rule is an invitation to perform it everywhere. Sentence conjunction, fragments, one-sentence paragraphs, profanity, anecdotes, headings and rhetorical questions remain subordinate to the article. An editing pass should leave the writing more intentional, not more visibly processed.

After every pass, ask what improved, what meaning or cadence was damaged, whether the supplied formulation was stronger, whether the result became more generic, and whether a local improvement harmed the whole. Revert any change without a defensible editorial benefit.

## 12A-inspired whole-site standard

The Portfolio uses current British Board of Film Classification 12A guidance as an external calibration point. It does not claim that the site has been submitted to, reviewed by or certified by the BBFC.

The whole public site is treated as one work. Review all public routes and copy together, not one article in isolation. The local language boundaries are:

- at most one site-wide use of `fuck`;
- uses of `shit` and `piss` are contextual editorial findings, not fabricated numeric failures, and must remain infrequent enough for the whole work plausibly to retain a 12A character;
- `cunt`, `twat` and `cock` are prohibited public terms;
- no swear exists merely for heat, filler, hostility or manufactured edge; and
- every swear earns its place through meaning, character, humour, quotation or emphasis.

Context, tone, repetition and cumulative effect matter more than counting alone. Review the complete 12A character, including dangerous behaviour, discrimination, drugs, nudity, sex, sexual violence or threat, suicide or self-harm, threat or horror, and violence whenever those categories are relevant.

This classification changes only through Harley's explicit editorial decision. Content volume, estimated reading time and hypothetical reader journeys do not change it automatically. Do not introduce runtime journey tracking to enforce the house standard.
