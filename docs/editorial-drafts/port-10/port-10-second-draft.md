# Rian Hughes turned up in my font shortlist

Chassis was already winning.

I was looking for a face for **The Usual Specialists**. The brief, at first, was pretty crude: solid caper, solid crew, solid word. **SPECIALISTS** needed to look like you could hang a tank off it and it wouldn’t budge.

As I remember it, Eurostile, Bank Gothic and Korolev were all in the running. Chassis was ahead. Then I noticed who designed it.

Rian Hughes.

That name had been in my head since I was ten.

I’d also read Hughes talking, years later, about the way he puts type and image together. Seeing his name under Chassis gave me a little *oh*. Right. Stop shopping for fonts. Look at the word.

I’d been asking whether the logo looked sturdy enough.

Better question: **how does the story earn that sturdiness?**

The Specialists do nearly all the dangerous work before Patch gets anywhere near the vault. Index maps the route. Silk tries to break it. Writ makes sure it’s lawful. Klause picks the plan. Rollback deals with whatever goes bang. Receipt makes sure nobody gets to rewrite history afterwards.

Do that lot properly and the caper itself becomes almost offensively boring.

That’s the joke in the mark. **THE USUAL** is small because, once this slab of specialists has done its job, it really is just the usual. **SPECIALISTS** is massive because the Specialists are the job.

I looked at Chassis again and it got silly quickly.

Silk could sling a rappel line around one of those cut-outs and disappear down the inside of a letter. Index could spread maps through the gaps and mark routes for Silk to try to break. If half the word blew up, Rollback could drag one of those great fat strokes across as a containment barrier. Klause could finish the whole thing with a big K stamp over whichever route survived.

I could stage the caper inside the word.

There was the answer.

Chassis itself also puts me in mind of those enormous old American cars. I keep wanting to say a sixties Oldsmobile and I’m probably wrong, but you know the sort of thing: rounded fenders, bonnet somewhere near your shoulder, more metal than seems strictly necessary.

The letters have that same big-bodied confidence, except Hughes has cut these enormous holes and voids through them. Mass with somewhere for the machinery to go.

No idea whether Hughes had cars anywhere near his head when he drew it. That’s just where mine went.

My first Rian Hughes memory is *2000 AD*, 1992, at the tail end of being ten.

*Tales from Beyond Science* looked angular as hell. Hard shapes, vivid blocks of colour, pages that didn’t look like anything else I was seeing in *2000 AD*. The stories had a bit of *The Twilight Zone* about them without feeling like somebody doing *The Twilight Zone* again, and some of them were pure nightmare fuel.

There was the bloke who works out what men’s nipples are for, cuts his eyes out, and can still see.

Then there were the red men who came out of mirrors and took you away if you slipped up and fell asleep with a mirror facing into the room.

That is an outrageous thing to put into a ten-year-old’s head.

I loved it.

I’ve reread *Tales from Beyond Science* every year or two ever since. Hughes never became one of those artists I remembered fondly and left in childhood. The pages still look brilliant to me.

Somewhere later on I read about him as a designer as well as a comic artist: type, logos, lettering, the whole lot. What grabbed me was how comprehensively he seemed to think about the page. Type wasn’t a label you stuck on after the picture was finished. It belonged to the picture.

That made something I’d loved at ten easier to name.

Then, thirty-odd years later, I’m staring at a font shortlist for a crew of autonomous agents and there’s his name again.

The funny bit is that Korolev is Hughes too. As I remember it, I’d already ruled Korolev out before I noticed that.

So much for destiny.

Chassis was winning before the name meant anything. The name reminded me to look properly at why.

**PATCH** ended up in ITC Serif Gothic Black. The strapline went its own way. Chassis stayed with The Usual Specialists. They were different jobs, so they got different answers.

Thirty-four years after those angular pages in *2000 AD*, I bought Chassis and built a word big enough for Silk to rappel through.

As I remember it, anyway.
