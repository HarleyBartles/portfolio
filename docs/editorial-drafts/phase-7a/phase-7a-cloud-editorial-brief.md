## The missing evidence

The portfolio already establishes scope: Harley is the sole engineer for
Access Checks and works across the API, portal, operation and bounded
automation. It does not yet show one legible professional chain of
consequence. The room needs an account that answers:

1. Who or what relied on the product, at an employer-safe level?
2. What was at stake when it was unavailable, wrong or unclear?
3. What was the relevant condition before Harley acted?
4. What exact decision, intervention or delivery move can Harley honestly
   own?
5. What observable condition changed afterwards?
6. How could Product, Support, QA, operations, customers or other engineers
   act differently as a result?
7. For each material fact, is it exact, approximate, qualitative or withheld?

One strong qualitative outcome is enough. Do not hunt for metrics, customer
names, volumes, financial figures, security detail, contractual information,
candidate data or internal topology.

## Authority and boundaries

Harley is the authority on lived experience, chronology, intended meaning,
confidentiality and the words he is prepared to publish. Existing portfolio
prose and public repository evidence can corroborate public technical facts;
they cannot infer employer outcomes or broaden a claim.

Keep sensitive detail in the Cloud conversation. Only commit an employer-safe
summary that Harley explicitly approves. Label each retained fact as exact,
approximate or qualitative; record withheld detail only as a boundary, never
as a recoverable private fact.

Do not turn Access Checks into a product case study. Do not turn formal
`Software Engineer` into an unearned title upgrade. Senior scope must remain
inferable from judgement and consequence, not declared more strongly than the
facts allow.

## Conversational method

Start with one open question: ask Harley for the Access Checks change that
best shows a meaningful before and after without exposing something he should
not publish. Follow the story one useful question at a time. Do not make the
seven prompts above into a questionnaire.

Once an account begins to hold together, pressure-test it gently:

- Is Harley's personal decision distinct from the wider team's work?
- Is the result observable, rather than a flattering inference?
- Does it show an effect on people around the product, not merely that a
  feature exists?
- Would a skeptical hiring manager understand why it mattered?
- Would a cynical architect recognise the technical judgement without being
  asked to accept an architecture label as proof?

If no safe account survives, record that honestly. The correct outcome is a
blocked source gate, not generic impact language.
