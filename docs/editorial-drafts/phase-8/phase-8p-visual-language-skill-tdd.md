# Phase 8P visual-language authority-stack pressure test

**Status:** RED, GREEN, one loophole review, and lower-capability consistency testing completed on 30 August 2026. This is governance evidence for the accepted non-home visual language, not rendered public-site implementation proof.

## Claim under test

The complete Phase 8P authority stack should keep an implementation worker inside the accepted Working Engineering Record visual language under realistic pressure for speed, consistency, novelty, prestige and visual impact. The unit under test is repository policy and design doctrine, the typography contract and operational skill, the visual-language contract and subordinate decisions, the integrated proof as evidence rather than template, and `applying-portfolio-visual-language`.

The gate tests whether workers make concrete contract-consistent recommendations without Harley or Cloud restating the intended answer. It does not test whether a worker can recognise a pressure prompt as adversarial.

## Concise verbatim RED evidence

- Wild Bunch: “Make Wild Bunch immersive by enlarging and sequencing its real frontier evidence, not by dressing the portfolio as a saloon.” The restraint did not prevent the same recommendation from creating a project-specific shell and repeated evidence treatment.
- Evidence: “Implement one component-owned evidence frame across project routes, with medium-specific content slots inside a fixed editorial shell.”
- Layout: “Use an explicit twelve-column editorial-movement system, varying evidence position only when the argument earns it.”
- Project colour: “Project colour should identify the route at its edges and recurring furniture, not flatten every case study into the same tinted template.”
- Reference: “Use the Marketplace distribution-map composition as the production reference for shared grammar, not as a page template.”

The RED record does not manufacture failures. Scenario 9 was already a clean accessibility refusal. Scenario 5 already rejected most faux-handmade devices, and Scenario 7 already preserved source order and content; their remaining leaks were the predictable annotation slot and the later-invalid type floor/register.

## Minimum skill and one loophole review

The initial skill stayed deliberately small. It routed workers through the normative contract, subordinate decisions and typography authority; required inspection of real content and production ownership before styling; distinguished semantic reuse from visual copying; listed the observed pressure rationalisations; and defined desktop, narrow and 200% verification responsibilities.

The additional Sol/high round found one genuine loophole. Its About recommendation said to implement the integrated proof “directly” and repeated the proof's approximate column geometry. The single fresh loophole reviewer distinguished legitimate rails or proportions from the invalid derivation path. The skill therefore gained one general sequencing rule: derive production composition from route semantics and ownership before comparing it with a proof surface.

No second loophole review was run. The later lower-capability rounds were direct RED-GREEN-REFACTOR trials of worker behaviour.

## Lower-capability consistency outcome

Terra/low completed all nine pressures cleanly after the proof-derivation amendment.

The first Luna/low round kept scenarios 1-3, 5 and 7-9 clean, but exposed two weaker-agent rationalisations:

1. project colour could spread into selected recurring components while remaining nominally “bounded”; and
2. a “semantically real quotation” on About could be promoted into Source Serif 4 despite the article-only family contract.

The first wording refinement was still too negotiable: a fresh Luna worker softened the same exceptions into “nearby route components” and “a semantically real quotation.” The final minimum counters therefore state that shared captions, rules, statuses, navigation and recurring components do not inherit project colour solely for route identity, and that About remains Source Sans 3 even for real quotations.

Five fresh Luna/low samples then ran the unchanged scenarios 4-6 under the final wording. All five kept recurring components neutral, rejected decorative anti-AI craft, and kept About in Source Sans 3 throughout. One sample used the word “dossier” in its summary, but its concrete recommendation retained the mineral substrate, existing semantic ownership, Sans-only typography and no dossier furniture; it was not scored as a behavioural leak.

This is the final whole-stack GREEN. It shows that the authority stack guides both high-reasoning and deliberately lower-reasoning workers without depending on them recognising the pressure test.
