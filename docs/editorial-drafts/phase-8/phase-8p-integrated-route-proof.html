Projects / Wild Bunch

Wild Bunch

A new Western game inspired by a childhood favourite, built around seeded worlds, exact replay and towns that remain themselves when the player rides on.

Pre-alpha. The project can justify complicated architecture only where the game actually needs the properties it buys.

Making chance reproducible

I treated the seed as the address of a starting world. Randomising it is a first-class player choice. Once chosen, that world can be revisited by tests or by me chasing a bug.

The map follows the same idea. A compact recipe produces meaningful route distances and more than one way through the map. Every road remains reproducible without being encoded separately.

Town layout closes a subtler gap. Every town comes from the world contract, and the chosen entropy policy decides whether salt may vary its layout. Once generated, that layout becomes session state and travels through snapshots and replay.

The seed describes the base world; it doesn't swallow the whole playthrough. Difficulty, entropy policy, named salts and player choices remain legible inputs of their own.

The warm field is finite and terminates back into mineral ground. The image crosses a local grid relationship because the evidence earns the interruption; the shell stays neutral.

Why ADRs?

A love letter to a future engineer.

Sometimes you're writing to your own future self.

You leave them enough of the argument to challenge your decision properly. The record serves whoever inherits the system, including when they decide the architecture should change.

I learned this by failing to write one.

I didn't know the documentation was me

I hadn't designed the system alone. I was early in my career, left holding the baton, completely ill-equipped to explain the complex system I knew like the back of my hand.

We made the user's job simpler by accepting more complexity into the system. Simplicity at the boundary was something the implementation had to earn.

The architecture wasn't theatre

Everybody thinks CQRS and event sourcing are theatre until somebody asks for a full audit history.

For plenty of systems, that criticism is fair. These patterns introduce concepts, infrastructure and failure modes that would be wasteful in a straightforward application. LENS had to reconstruct relevant actions and attribute them to an actor. The architecture was load-bearing.

Read the published article

Serif begins only at authored reading. Navigation, metadata and section mechanics remain Sans. The article is quieter than the project display and uses space rather than repeated rules for rhythm.
