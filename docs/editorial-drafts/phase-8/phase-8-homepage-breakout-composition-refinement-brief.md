## 1. Page-level spatial rule

The homepage should not force every movement through one polite central content rail.

The governing rule is:

> **Text belongs to an editorial gutter. Proof and spectacle are allowed to own the viewport edge.**

The intended page rhythm is now:

**Fold 1 ordered centre → Fold 2 breakout from the right → Fold 3 answering breakout from the left → Writing returns to ordered restraint → Patch takes full-width glory → professional close returns to ordered restraint.**

This alternation is deliberate. It is not licence for random full bleed or arbitrary edge-breaking. Breakout direction, crop, overlap, scale and return-to-order must be controlled, readable and breakpoint-authored.

The quiet movements become stronger because they return to the governed centre. The expressive movements become stronger because they are allowed to challenge it.

Do not give Fold 1, Writing or the professional close breakout treatment merely for consistency.

## 2. Fold 2 — Marketplace breaks in from the right

The accepted modular negative-space/channel system remains authority. Do not reopen the visual-language side quest or invent a replacement metaphor.

The current pass has the right ingredients but the wrong spatial relationship. Enlarging the visual enlarged its frame with it, so it still reads as a complete tilted diagram placed beside copy. The image and copy also remain separated by an implied no-man's-land, reinforced by a hard image frame.

The next composition should behave as **one shared editorial field**.

### Copy plane

- Keep the copy anchored from the **left side of the viewport** with a generous reading gutter. Text must never sit hard against the physical screen edge.
- The copy may sit on an opaque mineral plane/slab so readability remains effortless.
- That mineral copy plane is allowed — and encouraged — to **intrude into the visual field**.
- Treat the copy plane itself as a cropping device. Tiles may continue behind it and disappear beneath it.
- The reader should not perceive a constitutional border between `copy territory` and `image territory`.

### Visual field

- The modular system should enter from the **right viewport edge**.
- Remove the hard black image border / conventional framed-illustration treatment. It falsely communicates `do not cross this boundary`.
- The visual should feel like a close observation window into a much larger modular field, not a complete diagram comfortably contained by a box.
- Use a much tighter crop and substantially greater apparent scale.

As a starting proof, not a production constant:

- preserve roughly the current visual width;
- reduce the observation window toward roughly **50% of its current height**;
- scale the tile field roughly **180–220%** relative to the current composition.

The point is not to hit those numbers exactly. The point is to stop making the frame grow with the contents.

### Crop pressure

The crop should visibly interrupt the world.

Aim for tile geometry to be cropped or strongly pressured on **at least three of the four sides** of the observation field. Left/right cropping alone is insufficient; top and/or bottom should also visibly cut through the off-axis tile world.

The rectangular page/copy geometry and the tilted modular field should visibly disagree in a controlled way. That tension is useful.

### More world than route

The primary consequential route must remain legible, but the crop should imply more system beyond what is shown.

Add a small number of additional square modules where helpful. They do not all need to belong to the main path. Partial branches, detached pieces, or fragments entering/leaving the crop may establish that the visible route is one local section of a larger compatible system.

Do not create wallpaper. The composition should still be authored and restrained.

### Tilt

Keep the tilt modest. Test shallow values first. Crop and scale should do more expressive work than dramatic perspective or rotation.

Avoid turning the treatment into a literal board, game surface, faux-3D world or perspective spectacle.

### Fold 2 acceptance read

The reader should no longer see:

`copy column | framed tilted diagram`

The intended read is closer to:

`copy holds its editorial ground while a larger modular system breaks in from the right and continues beyond both the viewport and the copy plane.`

Preserve the existing inward `→` route and the separate next-plate `↓` route according to the settled navigation grammar.

## 3. Fold 3 — Wild Bunch answers from the left

The new proof topology is correct enough to preserve. Do not return to generic diagram discovery.

The remaining issue is spatial: the current composition still says `here is the diagram` and `here is what I have to say about it`.

Fold 3 should become the answering breakout to Fold 2.

### Proof field

- The proof should enter from the **left viewport edge**.
- Give it materially more physical authority. The history / mechanisms / State object should feel like a substantial receipt, not an illustration occupying a polite lane.
- Do not introduce a containing diagram frame simply to define an image area.
- Preserve all locked topology and directionality exactly.

### Copy plane

- Keep the headline/supporting copy on the **right side** with a healthy editorial gutter from the physical viewport edge.
- The copy remains clean, contemporary and easy to read.
- Do not make the copy look like a caption attached to a diagram.

### State is the seam

Column 3 — **State** — is the compositional bridge between proof and prose.

Push State rightward into the current no-man's-land. Its right-edge information-loss treatment should travel toward and into the copy territory rather than completing safely inside the proof object.

The intended visual sequence is continuous:

**immutable ordered history → Cache / Replay → same derived State → information quality breaks down → headline/claim emerges on clean mineral ground.**

The State fade therefore has a structural job. It is not merely a texture treatment on the rightmost box.

Where legibility permits, allow the headline to begin within the late-stage fade or immediately beside it so that the claim feels like the conclusion of the proof rather than separate commentary.

Do not put the entire page in sepia or Wild West styling. Warm/material character remains local to the proof; the surrounding homepage stays recognisably harleybartles.com.

### Topology remains non-negotiable

No arrow terminates at an event record.

- Events emit leftward reads into the shared replay spine.
- The replay spine runs down and beneath the history into Replay.
- Events emit rightward flows into Cache.
- Cache emits into State.
- Replay emits into the same State.

All meaningful labels remain real deterministic HTML/SVG text. Generated text is inadmissible.

Local Sol may use deterministic tooling, generative tooling, or a hybrid for material/fade exploration. If generative exploration is useful, the existing allowance remains up to **five genuine creative iterations of the desired visual**, not five arbitrary tool calls. Structural truth and final text remain deterministic.

### Fold 3 acceptance read

The reader should no longer see:

`diagram on the left | prose on the right`

The intended read is:

`the proof enters from the left, resolves into State, State materially dissolves into the claim on the right.`

Preserve the inward `→` route and the next-plate `↓` route as separate actions.

## 4. Writing — return to order

Do not redesign Writing.

Its strength is precisely its lack of visual furniture: writing is words, so words are what the homepage shows. The title, authored précis and negative space are the composition.

Keep the accepted ordered editorial field.

Only preserve the already settled clicker behaviour: the `#writing` jump target should land on the strongest self-contained Writing viewport composition rather than blindly coinciding with the section's DOM boundary. It does not need to show both horizontal section rules at once.

## 5. Patch — full-width glory, less web-polite decomposition

The accepted Patch assets, wordmarks, copy, narrative order and Klause overprint remain authority. Do not regenerate or redesign the episode.

The wide composition already has the desired attitude: simultaneous, boundary-breaking and materially full-width.

Intermediate and narrow decomposition currently retains the correct pieces but becomes slightly too web-friendly: repeated centred figures, common gutters and regular vertical spacing begin to read as a responsive image gallery.

Keep the decomposition. Change the **placement grammar**.

### Intermediate / narrow treatment

Use the existing pieces more assertively:

- unequal lateral positions rather than one shared gutter;
- unequal widths rather than one common responsive figure width;
- controlled left/right bias and alternation;
- selected panels pressing to or visually beyond a viewport edge;
- deliberate clipping by the viewport where compositionally useful;
- occasional tighter or slightly overlapping vertical relationships where the story benefits;
- preserve carrier rotations / physical edge character rather than regularising everything into web rectangles.

The intended feeling is a vertical comic-like sequence whose pieces claim the space they need, not a set of `<figure>` elements receiving identical slots.

Do **not** reconstruct the desktop collage on mobile. The authored sequential decomposition remains correct.

Klause remains a true zero-flow violation and must continue to bridge the folder / following-material boundary without changing document flow.

Controlled visual bleed is welcome. Actual horizontal page overflow is not.

### Patch acceptance read

Wide should remain full-width spectacle.

Intermediate/narrow should feel less like:

`centred panel → gutter → centred panel → gutter → centred panel`

and more like:

`authored sequential panels with unequal pressure, crop, scale and edge ownership.`

## 7. Navigation and jump-target contract remains settled

Directional language:

- `→` = go inward into the site for the roughly five-minute commitment;
- `↓` = continue to the next authored homepage plate;
- `↑` = optional return upward only where it genuinely helps without clutter.

No scrolljacking, snap scrolling or intercepted wheel/touch behaviour.

A jump target is an authored **viewpoint**, not necessarily a section boundary.

On wider/tablet layouts, native anchor jumps should land on the strongest self-contained viewport composition for that movement. Depending on the movement, that may include both section rules, one rule, or neither.

On narrow portrait layouts, a jump only needs to establish a clean authored start. Natural scrolling is expected and a cognitive movement may extend well beyond one physical screen.

## 8. Integration and objective checks

After the refinement, inspect the complete page again at the established four review widths and around the relevant breakpoint boundaries.

At minimum verify:

- no page-level horizontal overflow;
- breakout artwork may visually reach/cross viewport edges without creating horizontal scrolling;
- copy retains comfortable readable gutters and does not become visually noisy;
- Fold 2 still communicates base family + Superpowers+ intervention + viable route in approximately ten seconds;
- Fold 3 topology remains technically exact after spatial recomposition;
- Fold 3 State fade actually mediates the proof/copy seam;
- Writing remains visually untouched apart from anchor positioning/navigation grammar;
- Patch semantic title/order and zero-flow Klause behaviour remain intact;
- the professional close remains quiet and legible;
- native anchor jumps still land on authored viewpoints without scroll interception;
- reduced-motion and `?media=off` behaviour remain understandable where already supported.

Do not optimize for making every cognitive movement fit one physical viewport. That is only useful where the composition naturally earns it. Portrait/mobile is inherently scroll-led.
