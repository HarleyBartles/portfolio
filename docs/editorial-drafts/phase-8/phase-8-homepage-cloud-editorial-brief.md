# Phase 8 homepage handoff — Cloud editorial and art-direction room

**Status:** Active opening brief. Phase 8P has landed on `main` and is deployed. The deferred Phase 8 homepage work may now resume as a Cloud editorial/art-direction room. This brief does not authorize production implementation or a local JIT implementation plan.

**Opening base:** `b64f143fc3b8cfee07d6011bf30dceaaa956309b` — PR #47 merge commit.

## Why this room exists now

Phase 8 was deliberately interrupted before homepage implementation. The old homepage was materially weaker than the routes it pointed to, and its inherited warm-paper/copper/large-serif/tracked-label grammar had become a credibility risk because contemporary design culture could plausibly identify the combined surface as a common AI-generated aesthetic.

Phase 8P has now done the prerequisite work. The deployed non-home site establishes an accepted visual language: cool mineral substrate, Source Sans 3 as the shared site voice, Source Serif 4 only for authored longform, Source Code Pro only for genuine technical material, rectilinear alignment with rare evidence-earned interruption, artifact-first evidence, relationship-led cadence, a quiet single-flow shell, and bounded project-native colour when the material earns it.

The homepage is still intentionally the old surface. Its mismatch with the rest of the deployed site is expected evidence, not a regression to repair piecemeal. This room owns the deferred composition, editorial hierarchy and homepage-specific art direction that can now be designed against a proven site-wide system.

A Cloud adversarial review after the 8P deployment assessed the non-home work at roughly **£17k**, with a defensible respected-agency band around **£14k–£20k**, and found **no 50% AI-aesthetic penalty**. Treat that as calibration, not as a guaranteed floor or an invitation to inflate the homepage. The homepage must not drag the now-established system back toward generic personal-site or recognisable model-default presentation.

## The accepted 8P system is a constraint, not a homepage template

The homepage must belong to the same site without becoming a large copy of an interior route.

Protect the accepted semantic roles:

- Source Sans 3 remains the shared site voice.
- Source Serif 4 remains an authored-reading register, not a prestige effect.
- Source Code Pro remains technical evidence, not navigation, metadata or decorative engineering shorthand.
- Cool mineral remains the established shared substrate unless the homepage earns a bounded front-matter treatment that still clearly belongs to the same system.
- Project-native colour remains source-owned and bounded.
- Evidence remains artifact-first.
- Space expresses relationship.
- Shared shell identity and primary navigation remain quiet; route context belongs to content.

But do **not** copy the integrated 8P proof, existing interior grid ratios, project hero structures or non-home section patterns into `/` because they are available. The homepage has a different job and should derive its composition from that job.

A successful homepage may be more layered, more paced and more expressive than an interior page. Its extra expression must feel like the same publication opening rather than a separate microsite or a return to the old theme.

## Editorial-room method

Work conversationally. Ask **one consequential material question at a time**.

Do not make Harley answer a questionnaire and do not force choices between prematurely complete mockups. Start from what the homepage needs to accomplish, inspect the actual evidence available, and let the composition emerge through decisions.

For each material decision:

1. state the job the choice must do;
2. bring in source or rendered evidence when it changes the judgement;
3. offer materially different alternatives only when they are genuinely plausible;
4. name the trade-off and genericity/AI-convergence risk;
5. let Harley decide taste where the evidence does not decide it;
6. deliberately challenge the preferred answer once before closing it; and
7. persist the settled decision while it is still fresh.

Do not reopen a closed decision because another valid aesthetic exists. Reopen only when later integrated proof exposes an actual contradiction.

Use deterministic rendered comparison surfaces when a decision is spatial, typographic, motion-related or otherwise hard to judge honestly from prose. Make meaningful states URL-addressable where possible so Cloud can inspect them through Opera without depending on brittle interaction automation.

## Questions this room must actually resolve

The room is not complete until it has settled enough of the following that local Sol can plan without inventing design:

1. **Opening identity:** what exactly is said and shown in the first fold, in what order, and what is the stable professional consequence-bearing signal?
2. **Professional provenance:** how `5 years at The Access Group` appears without turning the employer into hero branding.
3. **First evidence doorway:** which substantive receipt is available within one click or one natural scroll, and how the architect/professional paths coexist without visible persona UI.
4. **Front-matter composition:** what spatial/art-direction language makes `/` feel like the opening of the accepted publication rather than another interior page.
5. **Homepage cadence:** how many major movements/spreads the page needs and what job each movement performs. Do not add a movement merely to fill a familiar portfolio template.
6. **Feature admission:** which project, writing and Patch material earns homepage space, whether any selection remains dynamic, and what each excerpt contributes before the click.
7. **Variation:** if anything changes between visits or sessions, what reader benefit justifies it and how completeness remains authoritative.
8. **Writing feature:** how authored longform appears on the homepage without importing the article register as decorative prestige.
9. **Patch:** how Patch acts as a signature/interlude without becoming the whole portfolio identity.
10. **Professional close:** how the page reaches About, CV, availability and contact without a sales-funnel ending or self-conscious portfolio commentary.
11. **Motion:** what opening/closing or fold/reveal behaviour is worth its complexity, what reduced-motion users receive, and what happens when motion is absent.
12. **Responsive composition:** how the design behaves at desktop, 768, 390, 320 and actual 200% zoom without becoming a desktop poster stacked vertically.
13. **Shell boundary:** whether the homepage uses any legitimate variation of the quiet masthead and how that variation transitions into the established interior shell.
14. **Asset needs:** whether existing project/evidence imagery is enough, whether one ambient asset is genuinely worth commissioning, and what custody applies.
15. **AI-convergence gate:** whether a credible current cultural source can explain the final homepage primarily as a model-default cluster. If yes, the valuation is halved and the design is not ready.
16. **Agency valuation:** whether the homepage sustains or improves the respected-agency quality established by the deployed non-home system instead of making the site feel cheaper at its most visible surface.

## Adversarial valuation gate

Use the portfolio policy's respected-agency lens throughout the room.

The automatic rule remains exact: if a credible contemporary source identifies the delivered **combined visual grammar** as a common AI-generated aesthetic, apply a 50% valuation penalty. Do not average the failure away because individual decisions are tasteful or because the engineering is strong.

Do not mechanically avoid every individual ingredient ever mentioned in an AI-tell article. The failure is culturally recognisable convergence and implausible human custody, not the presence of one serif, one warm artefact or one rectangular panel.

Also test the opposite failure: performatively handmade anti-AI styling can become its own generic posture. Handwriting, tape, grime, scrapbook collage, fake corrections and decorative imperfection are not evidence of humanity.

The homepage should make an evaluator think that an editor/designer had access to the actual material, made choices from it, and knew what to leave out.
