## The provisional article job

The Phase 7 design currently assigns Provisioning the environment-and-knowledge-architecture argument:

> More instructions do not make a better agent. Useful capability comes from putting the right knowledge at the right scope at the right time.

Its distinct job is to show experienced engineering judgement in the selection, placement, routing and maintenance of agent knowledge. It must not become a catalogue of Harley's plugins, skills or instruction files. This proposition is a starting hypothesis. Harley and Cloud Sol may replace it if discovery reveals a truer and stronger opinion while preserving the article's distinct place in the publication.

## What Why ADRs taught us

Why ADRs moved from a competent technical draft to a £9,300 article because discovery changed the substance, not because later sentences became shinier.

| Early state | Discovery that added editorial value | Lesson for Provisioning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A correct list of ADR responsibilities | Harley's failed LENS handover supplied the human need for decision records | Find the event, pressure or repeated failure that made the provisioning belief necessary |
| A general argument about architectural memory | “An ADR is a love letter to a future engineer” supplied the author's opinion | Agree what Harley believes before choosing the article structure |
| Architectural vocabulary as evidence | Career chronology, Mike's influence and Harley's actual implementation role established provenance without mythologising him | Establish where Harley learned or earned this judgement and refuse inflated framing |
| A UI example that initially looked like ordinary React work | Harley identified the policy aggregate and the human peer-review invariant | Follow examples down to the engineering decision that actually carries the argument |
| Remembered rationale presented too smoothly | Harley separated code-proven facts, first-party recollection and later inference | Classify every material claim before prose makes it sound equally certain |
| A historical lesson with no present proof | Public Wild Bunch ADR-0028 showed the discipline in current practice | Look for public, inspectable evidence that demonstrates the present-day behaviour |
| “You know DDD” risked sounding reluctant | Harley explained the intended invitation and the missing engineer-to-engineer exchange | Record anti-inferences: what a reasonable reader must not conclude accidentally |
| Pull quotes appeared as isolated decoration | Three voice-led quotes became a deliberate editorial rhythm and a design seed | Identify quotable Harley language during discovery; do not manufacture slogans afterwards |

Do not force Provisioning to imitate Why ADRs. It may need a different structure, amount of personal narrative or visual grammar. Reuse the discovery discipline, not the article shape.

## Story discovery with Harley

Begin by talking with Harley. Ask one focused question at a time and follow the answer rather than marching through a questionnaire. Do not draft the finished article while material questions remain open.

Discovery must establish:

1. What happened that first made accumulation feel like a systems problem rather than an untidy prompt.
2. Why each added instruction, skill, rule or context source seemed reasonable at the time.
3. What observable failure exposed the combined cost: hesitation, contradiction, routing churn, stale guidance, maintenance burden, lost task focus or something else.
4. What Harley changed and what happened afterwards.
5. Which conventional engineering judgement the change demonstrates: scope, ownership, dependency management, lazy selection, authority, cohesion, verification placement, YAGNI or another principle.
6. What remains unresolved in Harley's current practice. The article must not pretend provisioning is a solved automatic process if it still needs supervision.
7. Which examples are public and inspectable, which are first-party recollection, which are inference, and which must remain private.
8. What a weary hiring manager should understand differently about Harley after reading it.
9. What a cynical architect should be able to agree with quickly, even if they dislike agent-oriented terminology.
10. What Harley's actual opinion is, expressed in language he would naturally use.

Actively test for missing story layers. Ask what caused the problem, why it mattered, who or what carried the cost, what alternatives existed, why the chosen boundary was better, what would make Harley reconsider it, and what evidence could falsify the conclusion.

## Editorial contract to settle before final prose

Cloud Sol and Harley must agree all of the following in conversation:

- **Governing argument:** one falsifiable sentence that contains an opinion, not a topic label.
- **Publication job:** the distinct bill this article pays within the portfolio.
- **Opening pressure:** a concrete moment, tension or observable failure that earns the argument.
- **Engineering judgement:** the conventional engineering discipline demonstrated through agentic work.
- **Hiring-manager change:** what becomes easier to shortlist after reading.
- **Architect change:** what becomes easier to respect or agree with after reading.
- **Evidence route:** exact public proof where available, with first-party account and inference visibly separate.
- **Uncertainty and privacy:** facts that need qualification or exclusion.
- **Anti-inferences:** plausible but wrong readings the prose must not invite.
- **Voice anchors:** Harley phrases, rhythms, jokes or images worth preserving.
- **Pull-quote candidates:** only lines that emerge naturally from the argument.
- **Visual proposition:** what relationship a later principal figure might clarify. Cloud Sol proposes meaning, not React or CSS.
- **Reconsideration condition:** where the advice stops applying or what would change Harley's view.

## Copy standard

The settled manuscript must:

- read as an authored argument rather than a checklist wearing an article coat;
- preserve Harley's contractions, directness and judgement;
- make its strongest specific evidence available before a weary reader has to excavate for it;
- let a sceptical architect disagree with a choice without dismissing the reasoning;
- distinguish environment knowledge, workflow method, task intent and verification only where the distinction advances the story;
- avoid generic instructions-as-code analogy unless Harley's experience genuinely supports it;
- avoid stock AI contrast, symmetrical scaffolding and familiar comfort phrases when they flatten Harley's voice;
- remain clear after the fatigue pass; pattern evidence cannot overrule facts or intended meaning;
- avoid a universal “less context is always better” claim; selection and placement are the issue;
- state limitations, continuing manual judgement and unsolved parts honestly; and
- earn its place beside Why ADRs and Vibe without repeating either article's primary responsibility.
