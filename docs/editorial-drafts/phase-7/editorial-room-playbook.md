# Editorial-room playbook

This is the reusable method exercised during Phase 7. It is internal workflow
guidance, not public-copy rules and not a substitute for Harley's judgement.

## Authority and discovery

- Start with Harley's story, not a polished premise. Establish what happened, why it mattered, the evidence, the privacy boundary and what each reader should understand differently.
- Treat the seed thesis as an entry point. If discovery changes the argument, title or evidence hierarchy, move with it rather than protecting the opening brief.
- Protect earlier selves and other actors. Reflection should explain the old constraints, not make somebody look foolish so the later lesson lands harder.
- Verify load-bearing mechanisms and inspect primary evidence as soon as an anecdote becomes causal. Keep observed behaviour, Harley's inference and universal claims distinct.

## Persisting a Cloud room

Keep a repo-local answer log. Record material answers, corrections, evidence and privacy boundaries, editorial decisions, curveballs and valuable cutting-room threads while they are fresh. Commit a coherent checkpoint when the recovery value justifies interrupting discovery: a changed argument, a material factual boundary, a meaningful evidence batch, approaching context risk, or final handoff.

Do not commit every turn. Before a checkpoint, reconcile the log, manuscript and cross-article ledger so the commit gives the next editor a useful recovery point.

## Testing the governing argument

Use the hot-take pressure test when an article has good material but only a descriptive thesis:

> I say X; fight me.

Reader-facing, that becomes: **bold claim; show me.** The device is for finding a proposition a competent reader could dispute and the evidence needed to earn it. It is not an instruction to manufacture controversy.

After the evidence exists, one deliberate curveball can test whether the same material supports a sharper governing argument. Keep it only when it materially improves the article; do not add contrarian energy as decoration.

## Moving from discovery to production

1. Wait to rewrite until the lived story, governing argument, evidence boundary and obvious counterargument are stable.
2. Before sentence-level polishing, review paragraph jobs. Merge adjacent paragraphs carrying the same thought; isolate a short paragraph only when the separation changes its meaning.
3. Reconcile title, summary, opening, headings and closing as one promise. They must cash the same article.
4. Run the £10k-agency, weary-hiring-manager and cynical-architect lenses on the coherent manuscript, not a promising fragment.
5. Run the installed writing workflow. Pattern findings are evidence for editorial judgement, never forbidden-phrase rules or automatic rewrites.
6. Hand local production a settled manuscript, source/privacy custody, unresolved boundaries, valuable cut material and a clear read order. It does not need page composition before the handoff.

## Closing the room

Begin closure around fifteen minutes before a three-hour room ends. Do not open a new discovery branch unless it corrects a material factual error. Reconcile the thesis, title, lenses, cutting-room material and custody, then save a durable handoff.

Once a production article settles, retain the discovery record and cross-article material that future work needs. Archive rough working material under its article record instead of letting it compete with the current manuscript.
