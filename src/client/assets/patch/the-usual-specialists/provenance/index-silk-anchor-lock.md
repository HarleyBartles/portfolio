## Original commission intent

The mineral-anchor system was intended to make chapter boundary direction changes physically legible in the mineral page: independent rope pieces overlap beneath a compact wall fixing, and the fixing explains the tension change while sharing plaster/brick material grammar with Silk apertures.

## Material changes from original intent

The accepted Index→Silk boundary evolved into a purpose-built three-layer lock rather than a generic anchor overlay. It uses one generated damaged steel ring, a separate generated foreground knot constrained by an authored geometry guide, and a deterministic foreground ring arc that repaints steel over the knot to establish real front/back passage through the aperture.

## Accepted execution brief

Evidence status: `recovered-from-linear-record`.

Anchor: create a compact battered steel wall ring fixed into the cool-mineral page with local chipped plaster and restrained masonry exposure, genuine aperture and useful foreground/rear metal edges, transparent exterior and no long rope route. Knot: create only the red foreground rope geometry from the supplied deterministic guide; the red line alone is generation authority while grayscale wall/anchor/annotations are context. Match accepted rope material, form a compact loaded hitch through the ring, and generate no wall, steel ring or long chapter rope.

## Iteration history

The earlier broad anchor exploration was not promoted. A compact ring/wound was accepted for live integration. The first foreground-knot attempt did not survive. The accepted knot was then deterministically cropped to the useful contact geometry, and a deterministic foreground ring arc was derived from the anchor so the final composite could re-occlude the rope naturally.

## Acceptance decision

Harley Bartles accepted the generated anchor and then the complete composed lock on 2026-09-13 after in-browser review. The locked physical read is: Index rope enters the lock; authored knot wraps/crosses the steel; foreground ring arc repaints over the knot; Silk rope emerges from behind the lower ring.
