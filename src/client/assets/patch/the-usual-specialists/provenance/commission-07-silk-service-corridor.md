## Original commission intent

Create the world behind Silk's second structural breach: the grimier sub-wall service level of the same old apartment building as the safehouse. The image should show a practical route through old building infrastructure, carry restrained evidence that Silk has used the space before as part of her test rig, and provide enough real image area for the same moving world plate to serve both landscape and portrait frame viewports.

## Material changes from original intent

The earlier PORT-16 wording anticipated a separate compact/mobile master and a more generic service-void composition. Page geometry subsequently proved a stronger contract: one image must serve both the landscape and portrait Commission 07 frames, just as Commission 05 uses one world plate across frame orientations. The portrait treatment therefore crops and scales the same source rather than using a separate mobile scene.

An initial 2026-09-15 generation was reviewed but not selected because its composition was portrait-native and made the landscape crop sacrifice too much of the Silk-specific bulb-string cue. Harley then approved a second, landscape-native brief with a lower ceiling and a deliberately portrait-safe central story column. Only that second result entered repository custody.

## Accepted execution brief

Evidence status: `verbatim-recovered`.

> Create one photorealistic environment/depth plate for Silk’s second breach. This version should be composed as a landscape image first, with enough width to feel naturally expansive in the landscape aperture, while keeping the essential story beat inside a central portrait-safe column so the same source can still crop successfully into the portrait frame.
>
> The space is the hidden basement/service level of the same old apartment building as the safehouse. It should feel like Silk has moved out of the apartment and into the building’s fabric: old brick walls, rough concrete floor, exposed aging pipes, ducts, conduit, valves, brackets and practical building services.
>
> The room must feel noticeably more vertically cramped than the previous version. Lower the ceiling substantially. A person should be able to move through it only hunched, with overhead pipework and ducting close enough to make the compression obvious. Avoid a tall tunnel-like read. The ceiling should feel oppressive and useful, not cavernous.
>
> The landscape composition should use that extra width to show more of the service infrastructure laterally: heavier services along one side, secondary services along the other, and a narrow usable route through the middle. The width should make the space feel embedded in the building, not simply like a tall corridor turned sideways.
>
> The depth remains strong but finite. The eye follows the route toward a far turn where the service corridor bends around a corner and disappears. That turn must remain the main visual destination.
>
> A brighter practical light spills from around that unseen corner. The source stays hidden. It should imply Silk has set up a larger mobile work light just beyond the turn for some test requiring more illumination. The spill should pull the eye toward the bend without reading as daylight, an exit, or a theatrical mystery light.
>
> Silk’s prior use of the space should remain restrained:
> - a temporary string of bare utility bulbs added along the low ceiling;
> - one subtle red laser/survey line left over from a previous test;
> - perhaps a few small clamps or attachment points integrated into existing structure.
>
> Those details should not become the subject. The bulbs are practical infrastructure Silk installed for repeated use. The laser line is residue from a separate prior test.
>
> Do not include Silk or any active testing moment.
>
> Composition contract: this is one source image for both landscape and portrait apertures.
>
> The landscape crop is the primary composition. It should feel complete and intentional at wide aspect ratio, with useful lateral infrastructure and breathing room around the route.
>
> However, the central protected column must also stand alone as the portrait crop. Inside that central column, preserve:
> - the corridor route;
> - the low-ceiling read;
> - at least part of the bulb string;
> - the red test line;
> - the far turn;
> - the brighter spill from around the corner.
>
> Peripheral pipes, ducts and service clutter may be lost in the portrait crop. The essential narrative may not depend on anything near the far left or far right.
>
> The source should also contain generous real-environment overscan on all sides for the same ±64px parallax movement as Aperture 1. No empty padding, fades or artificial borders; the environment must simply continue beyond the useful crop.
>
> Avoid a strong extreme-foreground object. Keep the nearest services moderate enough that moving the whole image as one parallax plane still feels plausible.
>
> Do not generate the broken plaster frame, aperture rim, page surface, rope, anchors, typography, Receipt, decorative borders, or any baked-in hole. Environment only.
>
> Reject if it reads as:
> - tall or cavernous;
> - sewer, bunker, cave or dungeon;
> - sci-fi/cyberpunk;
> - clean modern plant room;
> - endless tunnel;
> - dramatic destruction;
> - generic industrial stock photography with no sense of Silk’s prior use.
>
> The main change from the first pass is therefore: wider, lower, more compressed, and composed natively for landscape, with a deliberately protected portrait-safe centre.
