## Original commission intent

Commission 09 is the final Silk cell and should cash in the established aperture language with a substantial destructive knock-through. The destruction level is closer to the first, heavily broken aperture than a restrained opening. The top edge is an exposed structural steel joist / beam revealed by demolition. One Acrow-style steel prop at each side supports that beam. The demolition goes too low at the bottom, exposing fractured floorboards and dusty space beneath the floor. The frame should feel like believable temporary structural engineering discovered through demolition, not an industrial-themed decorative border. The physical frame is separate from the later Commission 09 world imagery.

## Material changes from original intent

Harley supplied exactly two damaged-wall reference images for the landscape and limited their authority to demolition/material language: ragged knock-through edges, visible construction depth, fractured plaster, exposed underlying material and a generous central aperture. Their exact brick construction and silhouettes were not to be copied. The landscape frame also received a strict rectangular occlusion contract so a separately composited rectangular world can sit behind the foreground without any raw rectangular edge showing through. Transparency was made explicit both through the central aperture and outside the external broken-wall silhouette. Surviving plaster substrate was fixed to Portfolio mineral `#E6EAEB` / RGB `230, 234, 235`. The generated landscape arrived at the requested outer canvas of `1672 × 941`.

## Known accepted limitations

The images are accepted as generated; promotion did not authorise or perform source editing. For the landscape, alpha analysis found that arbitrary inset rectangles do not all have equally deep opaque coverage, so React uses the measured safe viewport rather than guessing from the visible aperture. An earlier rectangle (`x=240..1400`, `y=100..780`) fell into transparency by an 8px inward band and is not authorised for live use. The stronger measured landscape rectangle is `x=120..1540`, `y=90..860`, width `1420`, height `770`. Its inward perimeter minimum alpha is `251` at 8px, `251` at 12px, `250` at 16px and `250` at 20px.

For the portrait, React uses `x=99..851`, `y=129..1531`, width `752`, height `1402`. Measured perimeter minimum alpha is `250` at the viewport boundary, `250` at 4px inward, `251` at 8px inward and `250` at 12px inward; it falls to `133` at 16px and reaches transparency by 20px. The authorised live portrait viewport therefore claims a 12px inward alpha-250 safety band, no more.

## Composition / ownership contract

The generated frame assets own only the physical foreground frame: broken mineral-plastered wall, exposed structural steel beam, Acrow props, damaged floorboards and exposed construction/debris integral to the frame. React owns the rectangular world, clipped viewport, viewport geometry, world overscan, parallax motion, deterministic striped stand-in art, the stand-in world's `2px` cyan rectangular border, whole-composition placement, responsive size and page z-order. The hierarchy is `world <- viewport <- frame`. Only the **world** moves. The **viewport** and **frame** remain page-locked. The striped world is diagnostic, not final Commission 09 art. If its cyan rectangular edge becomes visible through the foreground frame, the occlusion contract has failed. At 390 CSS pixels and above React retains the accepted landscape frame and landscape viewport unchanged. At 389 CSS pixels and below React switches the composition aspect ratio, viewport and foreground source to the portrait treatment; the same striped world remains behind it with increased overscan and horizontal bleed for parallax headroom. The generated sources must not be edited to bake in the world, diagnostic border, final scene or page geometry.
