## Why it exists

Agentic Learning Lab is a curriculum for teaching non-coders to direct agents effectively. The learner directs work, inspects it, questions it, and directs again; they do not become the manual implementation layer for the agent.

## My role

I design the curriculum, exercises, safety boundaries, and recovery paths. The work sits between product design, technical teaching, and the practical engineering of disposable environments where mistakes are useful rather than costly.

## What works now

The earlier labs are usable and have been tested with real learners. They cover conversation versus workspace, project access, durable state, source-control save points, model versus harness, and tools and skills.

Each exercise gives the learner a bounded problem, a safe environment, and an explicit way back when an experiment fails.

## Decisions and trade-offs

The course avoids teaching prompt incantations. It teaches a durable working loop instead: direct, inspect, question, and redirect. That transfers across tools, but it requires more care than a catalogue of impressive-looking prompts.

## What remains

Later modules on context, provisioning, specialist sub-agents, and workflow design are still being drafted. The project is incomplete and improving through use rather than being presented as a finished curriculum.
